Does your restaurant need a website if you're on Swiggy and Zomato?

Why restaurants and cafés in India still benefit from their own website alongside Swiggy, Zomato and Instagram, what to put on it, and how it helps with direct orders, bookings and Google Maps.

Kavin Arulraj·7 min read·3 Nov 2026
Contents

  2. What a restaurant website does well
  3. What to put on a restaurant website
  4. Mistakes to avoid
  5. Keep it simple

If your restaurant is listed on Swiggy and Zomato and has an active Instagram, a website can seem unnecessary. Delivery apps bring orders, and Instagram brings attention. But a simple website still does several jobs those platforms can't, and some of them directly save you money.

What delivery apps don't do for you

  • You don't own the customer. The platform owns the relationship and the data. You can't easily reach the customer again.
  • Commissions on every order. Repeat customers who would happily order directly still go through the app.
  • Limited control of your story. Your page looks like every other listing, ranked by the platform's rules.
  • No dine-in or events focus. Apps are built for delivery, not table bookings, private dining, catering or events.

What a restaurant website does well

Answers the questions people Google

Before visiting, people search for your menu, prices, timings, location, parking and whether you take reservations. A website with this information, plus your Google Business Profile, answers them in seconds.

Brings in direct orders and bookings

A WhatsApp ordering button or a simple order form lets regulars order directly for pickup or your own delivery, with no commission. Table bookings and party enquiries can come through the same channel.

Wins catering, events and corporate orders

Office lunches, birthday parties and catering are high-value orders, and the people placing them usually check for a proper website and menu before calling.

Helps you rank on Google Maps

A website linked to your Google Business Profile, with your address and menu, helps Google understand and trust your restaurant, which supports your map ranking for "restaurants near me" searches.

What to put on a restaurant website

  1. Home: cuisine, location, timings and buttons to call, WhatsApp, book a table or order.
  2. Menu: as real text, not just a PDF or photo. Text menus load faster, read better on phones and can appear in Google searches for dishes.
  3. Photos: real photos of food, interiors and the team.
  4. Location and timings: map, parking, landmarks, holiday hours.
  5. Catering and events: if you offer them, a page with packages and an enquiry form.
  6. Links to your Swiggy, Zomato and Instagram pages for people who prefer them.

Mistakes to avoid

  • A menu only as a large image or PDF that's slow to open on mobile data
  • Outdated prices or timings, which annoy customers more than having no website
  • Auto-playing videos and heavy animations that make the site slow
  • No clear way to call or message from the first screen

Keep it simple

Most restaurants don't need a complex website or a full online ordering system on day one. A fast, four or five page site with a text menu, photos, map and WhatsApp ordering covers the essentials and fits a small business budget. Want the full argument for a website alongside social media?
