Shopify vs WooCommerce for Indian brands: which should you choose?

An honest comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce for Indian sellers: costs in rupees, UPI and COD, GST invoices, shipping, speed and SEO, and which one suits your store.

Shopify and WooCommerce run a huge share of India's independent online stores. Both can take UPI, cards and cash on delivery, both can rank on Google, and both can grow with you. The right choice depends less on which is "better" and more on how much you want to manage yourself.

The one-line difference

Shopify is a hosted platform: you pay a monthly fee and Shopify handles hosting, security, updates and checkout. WooCommerce is a free plugin for WordPress: the software costs nothing, but you arrange hosting and maintenance yourself or pay someone to.

Cost in India

This is where most comparisons mislead, because "free" WooCommerce is rarely free and Shopify's plan fee is only part of its cost.

For a small store, total yearly running costs often end up in the same range. Shopify's costs are predictable and monthly. WooCommerce's are lumpier: cheap months, then a developer bill when something breaks.

Payments: UPI, cards and COD

Both platforms work with the gateways Indian shoppers expect, including Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree and PhonePe, and both support cash on delivery. On Shopify, check whether your chosen gateway adds a Shopify transaction fee on your plan. On WooCommerce, gateways are plugins, so setup is flexible but you are responsible for keeping them updated.

COD is where Indian stores differ from global ones. Neither platform charges COD fees natively in a flexible way; both rely on apps or plugins to add a COD charge or restrict COD by pincode or order value.

GST invoices and tax setup

Indian stores need GST-compliant invoices with your GSTIN, HSN codes and the right split of CGST and SGST (same-state orders) or IGST (other states). Neither platform does this perfectly out of the box:

  • Shopify: tax overrides per state can be configured, and most Indian stores add a GST invoice app.
  • WooCommerce: tax tables are flexible, and GST invoice plugins are widely available.

Either way, test a few orders and have your CA check the invoices before launch.

Shipping

Shiprocket, Delhivery and similar aggregators integrate with both. Shopify's integrations tend to be quicker to set up; WooCommerce's can be customised further, for example special rules for heavy items or specific pincodes.

Speed and mobile performance

Most Indian shoppers browse on phones, often on patchy connections, so speed directly affects sales.

  • Shopify is fast by default thanks to its global infrastructure, but every app you install can add scripts that slow the store down.
  • WooCommerce speed depends heavily on your hosting. On cheap shared hosting it is often slow; on good hosting with caching it can be very fast.

SEO

Both can rank well. WooCommerce gives you full control of URLs and technical SEO. Shopify has fixed URL patterns such as /products/ and /collections/, which doesn't stop stores ranking but limits some customisation. Content, product descriptions, speed and links matter far more than the platform.

Ease of running the store

If you or your team will add products, run discounts and process orders yourselves, Shopify's admin is easier to learn. WooCommerce's admin is capable but feels more technical, and plugin updates occasionally need attention.

Which should you choose?

Choose Shopify if

  • You want to launch quickly and not think about servers.
  • You don't have a developer on call.
  • Your catalogue and checkout are fairly standard.
  • You plan to grow fast and need reliability during sales and launches.

Choose WooCommerce if

  • You already run a WordPress site with content that ranks.
  • You need unusual pricing, product options or B2B features.
  • You want to avoid monthly platform and app fees and have someone to maintain the site.
  • You want complete control of your data and code.

Our recommendation for most Indian D2C brands

For a new brand with up to a few hundred products, Shopify is usually the safer choice: faster to launch, less maintenance, and fewer things to break during a sale. WooCommerce makes more sense when content and customisation are central to the business, or when you already have a WordPress site.
