How many pages does a small business website need?

How many pages a small business website really needs, which pages matter most, why one page per service helps SEO, and when a one-page site is enough.

"How many pages do I need?" is one of the first questions in any website project, and it directly affects price. The honest answer: as many as your customers need to decide, and as few as you can keep up to date. For most small businesses, that's between five and fifteen.

The core pages almost every business needs

  1. Home: what you do, who it's for, why choose you, and how to get in touch.
  2. Services or products: one page per main service is ideal (more on that below).
  3. About: your story, team and credentials.
  4. Work, portfolio or gallery: proof that you do good work.
  5. Contact: form, WhatsApp, phone, map and timings.
  6. Privacy policy and terms: needed for forms, analytics and payment gateways.

That's a solid five-to-seven page site, enough for many local businesses.

Why one page per service helps

A single "Services" page listing everything is convenient, but it can only rank well for a few searches. A dedicated page for each main service, such as "Kitchen interiors", "Bedroom wardrobes" or "Full home interiors", can each rank for its own searches and answer that service's specific questions: price, process, timeline and examples.

If you offer four core services, that alone takes the site to around nine or ten pages, and it's usually the best investment in the whole project.

Pages that add value as you grow

  • FAQ: answers common questions and reduces repetitive calls.
  • Pricing or packages: filters enquiries and builds trust.
  • Case studies: detailed stories of projects, great for higher-value services.
  • Location pages: only if you genuinely serve distinct areas and can write useful content for each.
  • Blog: articles answering customer questions, which bring in search traffic over time.
  • Careers: if you hire regularly.

When a one-page website is enough

  • You offer one main service or product.
  • You're starting out and need something live quickly.
  • The site is mainly for people who already know you (referrals, Instagram followers) to check you out.
  • It's a landing page for an ad campaign.

One-page sites are fast and cheap, but limited for Google: they can't target many different searches. Plan to add service pages later if search traffic matters to you.

Signs you have too many pages

  • Pages with only a sentence or two of content
  • Several pages that say almost the same thing with a different area name
  • Outdated pages nobody has touched in years

Thin and duplicate pages don't help rankings and can make the whole site look neglected. Merge or remove them.

A typical plan by business type

BusinessTypical pages
Freelancer or consultant1–6
Clinic, salon or local shop5–8
Service business with several services8–15
Multi-service or B2B company15–30
