Freelancer vs agency: who should build your business website?

The real trade-offs between hiring a freelancer, a small studio or an agency to build your website in India: cost, speed, quality, support and risk, and how to choose.

Once you decide to get a website, the next question is who should build it. In India you can choose between a solo freelancer, a small studio, or a larger agency. Each can do excellent work, and each can go wrong. Here's how to pick the right one for your project.

The quick comparison

FreelancerSmall studioAgency
Typical costLowestMiddleHighest
Who you talk toThe person doing the workUsually the founder or leadAccount or project manager
SkillsOne person's rangeDesign, development and SEO in a small teamSpecialists for each area
SpeedDepends on their other workGood for small and mid projectsGood, but more process
Risk if someone leavesHighMediumLow
Best forSmall, clearly defined sitesBusiness sites and storesLarge, complex projects

When a freelancer is the right choice

  • Your site is small: a landing page, portfolio or five-page business site.
  • Your budget is tight and you can be flexible on timeline.
  • You've seen their previous work and it matches what you want.

The main risks are availability and continuity. A freelancer juggling several clients may go quiet for a week, and if they stop freelancing, you need someone else to understand your site. Reduce the risk by keeping your domain, hosting and logins in your own name from day one.

When an agency is the right choice

  • Your project is large: many pages, complex integrations, user accounts or a web app.
  • You need several specialists at once, such as UX, content, development and marketing.
  • You need formal processes, contracts and guaranteed availability.

You pay for the structure. Make sure you know who will actually work on your project; at some agencies the senior person pitches and a junior team builds.

Where a small studio fits

A small studio combines direct contact with the people doing the work and enough range to cover design, development and SEO properly. For most small and mid-sized businesses, such as a 6–15 page website or a store with a few hundred products, this is often the best balance of cost, quality and reliability.

Questions to ask whoever you hire

  1. Can I see three live websites you built, similar to mine?
  2. Who will design and build my site, and who do I contact?
  3. Is the price fixed? What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
  4. How many revision rounds are included?
  5. Will the domain, hosting and site be registered in my name?
  6. Will I be able to edit content myself? Is training included?
  7. What support do I get after launch, and what does it cost afterwards?
  8. How will you set the site up for Google (SEO, speed, Search Console)?

Clear, specific answers are a good sign. Vague answers on ownership and support are a red flag whoever you hire.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed "first page of Google" rankings
  • No portfolio of live sites, only screenshots
  • Unwilling to put scope and price in writing
  • Domain or hosting registered in their name "for convenience"
  • 100% payment upfront for a large project

The bottom line

Choose based on the size and risk of your project, not just the price. A small site from a good freelancer is great value. A complex store from a freelancer who disappears mid-project is expensive.
