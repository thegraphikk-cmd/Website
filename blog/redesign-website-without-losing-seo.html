Redesigning a website without losing your Google rankings

A checklist for redesigning or rebuilding a website while keeping your Google rankings: crawling the old site, keeping URLs, 301 redirect maps, content, launch-day checks and monitoring.

Kavin Arulraj·8 min read·9 Nov 2026

A redesign should bring more customers, not fewer. Yet many businesses launch a beautiful new website and watch their Google traffic drop by half the following month. It's almost always avoidable. Rankings are lost when pages disappear, URLs change without redirects, or content gets cut. Here's how to protect them.

Before you redesign: record what you have

  1. List every URL on the current site. Use a crawler tool, your sitemap, and Google Search Console's page indexing report.
  2. Find the pages that matter. In Search Console, check which pages get the most clicks and which search queries bring them. These pages need the most care.
  3. Note which pages have backlinks from other sites. You don't want those links pointing at error pages.
  4. Save a copy of the old site, or at least its titles, descriptions and content.
  5. Record current performance: clicks, top queries, key rankings and enquiries, so you can compare after launch.

During the redesign

Keep URLs the same where you can

The safest redesign changes how pages look, not where they live. If /services/kitchen-interiors ranks well today, keep that URL.

Don't cut content that ranks

Designers love minimal pages, but a page ranking for "modular kitchen cost Chennai" ranks because of its content. Keep the useful text, headings and FAQs, even if you restyle them.

Keep titles and meta descriptions

Carry over page titles and descriptions for important pages, improving them only where you're confident.

Keep internal links

Make sure important pages are still linked from the menu or other pages, not hidden.

The 301 redirect map

When a URL must change, a 301 redirect tells Google the page has permanently moved and passes most of its value to the new address. Build a spreadsheet:

Old URLNew URL
/about-us.html/about
/services.php?id=3/services/kitchen-interiors
/blog/2019/old-post/blog/updated-post

Redirect every old URL to its closest matching new page, not everything to the home page. Redirect chains (A to B to C) should be collapsed into one step.

Launch-day checklist

  • ☐ Redirects live and tested for every old URL
  • ☐ No "noindex" left over from the staging site
  • ☐ robots.txt allows crawling
  • ☐ New XML sitemap submitted in Search Console
  • ☐ Analytics and conversion tracking working
  • ☐ Forms, WhatsApp and call buttons tested
  • ☐ Site checked on mobile for speed and layout

After launch

  • Watch Search Console daily for the first two weeks for 404 errors and indexing problems.
  • Request indexing for your most important pages.
  • Compare clicks and rankings with your "before" numbers after 4–8 weeks.

Small fluctuations in the first few weeks are normal while Google processes the changes. A large, sustained drop usually points to missing redirects or removed content, both of which can be fixed.

Changing domains too?

Moving to a new domain at the same time adds risk. Redirect every old URL to the new domain and use the Change of Address tool in Search Console. If you can, change the design first and the domain later, so you can tell which change caused any issue.
