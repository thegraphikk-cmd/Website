How to get more Google reviews for your business (without breaking the rules)

Practical ways for Indian businesses to get more Google reviews: your review link, when and how to ask, WhatsApp templates, replying to reviews, and what Google's rules forbid.

Reviews are one of the biggest reasons a business shows up in the Google Maps results for searches like "dentist near me" or "caterers in Chennai". They also decide whether people call you or the next business on the list. The good news: most happy customers will leave a review if you make it easy and ask at the right moment.

Why reviews matter so much

  • Local ranking: Google uses the number, recency and rating of reviews as signals for the local map results.
  • Click-through: a business with 4.6 stars from 80 reviews gets chosen over one with 5 stars from 3.
  • Keywords: when customers mention a service ("root canal", "bridal makeup"), it helps Google connect you with those searches.

If you haven't set up your profile yet, start with how to add your business to Google Maps.

Step 1: Get your review link

Search for your business on Google while logged in to the account that manages it. In your Business Profile panel, choose Ask for reviews (or "Get more reviews"). Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly. Save it somewhere you can paste it quickly.

You can also print it as a QR code. Google offers one in the same panel, and it's useful at the counter, on bills or on a table stand.

Step 2: Ask at the right moment

Ask when the customer is happiest, not weeks later:

  • Clinics: right after a successful appointment or follow-up.
  • Salons: when the client is admiring the result in the mirror.
  • Shops and online stores: a day or two after delivery.
  • Service businesses: when you hand over the finished job or they thank you.

Asking in person first, then sending the link, works far better than the link alone.

Step 3: Send a short WhatsApp message

Most Indian customers will review from their phone. Keep the message short and personal:

Hi Priya, thank you for coming in today! If you have a minute, a quick Google review would really help our small clinic: [your link]. Thank you, Dr. Meena

Use their name, mention what you did, and send it the same day. One gentle reminder after a few days is fine; more than that feels pushy.

Step 4: Make it part of your routine

  • Add the link to your invoice, receipt and email signature.
  • Put a QR code card at the billing counter.
  • Add a "Review us on Google" button to your website's contact page.
  • Train staff to ask, and track how many reviews come in each month.

A steady trickle of reviews is better than fifty in one week and none after. Recency matters to customers and to Google.

What Google's rules don't allow

Breaking these can get reviews removed or your profile suspended:

  • Offering anything in exchange: no discounts, freebies or contest entries for reviews.
  • Review gating: you can't ask only happy customers, or filter people through a "were you satisfied?" step first.
  • Fake or bought reviews, including from staff, family or review sellers.
  • Reviewing from your own premises on a shop device. Let customers use their own phones.

Asking everyone and accepting the occasional 3-star review is safer, and it looks more genuine to customers.

Reply to every review

Replies show future customers that you care, and they encourage more people to write. Keep them short:

  • Positive: thank them by name and mention the service. "Thank you, Arjun! Glad the website redesign is bringing in more enquiries."
  • Negative: stay calm, apologise for the experience, and move the details offline. "We're sorry about the delay, Ravi. Please call us on [number] so we can make this right."

Never argue in public. People reading the reply matter more than the person who left the review.

What about fake negative reviews?

If a review breaks Google's policies (spam, a competitor, someone who was never a customer), you can report it from your Business Profile. Removal isn't guaranteed. A calm reply and a steady stream of genuine reviews limit the damage either way.

A realistic target

For most local businesses, getting 4 to 8 new reviews a month puts you ahead of most competitors within a year. Combine it with the rest of our local SEO checklist and the results compound.
