How to speed up a Shopify store on mobile

Practical ways to make a slow Shopify store faster on mobile: auditing apps, compressing images, simplifying the theme, cutting third-party scripts and measuring the results.

Shopify's servers are fast. When a Shopify store is slow, the cause is almost always something added on top: apps, heavy themes, huge images and tracking scripts. The good news is that most of these are fixable without rebuilding the store. Here's where to start.

Measure first

  • Run your home page, a collection page and a product page through PageSpeed Insights on mobile.
  • Check Shopify's Online Store → Themes speed report, if available for your store.
  • Open your store on your own phone with Wi-Fi turned off.

Write down the scores and how long the main image takes to appear, so you can see what each fix achieves. Our Core Web Vitals guide explains the metrics.

1. Audit your apps

Every app can add its own JavaScript and CSS to every page, and uninstalled apps sometimes leave code behind in the theme.

  • List every installed app and ask: do we still use it, and is it worth the speed cost?
  • Replace several single-purpose apps with one that does the job, or with built-in Shopify features.
  • After uninstalling an app, check the theme code for leftover snippets (or ask a developer).

2. Fix your images

  • Upload product photos at a sensible size, around 2000 pixels on the longest side; Shopify creates smaller versions automatically when the theme uses them correctly.
  • Compress images before uploading.
  • Make sure the theme serves responsive images, so phones don't download desktop-sized files.
  • Don't lazy-load the main hero or first product image; do lazy-load images further down the page.

3. Simplify the home page

  • Replace image sliders and carousels with one strong hero image. Most shoppers never see slide two anyway.
  • Avoid auto-playing background videos on mobile.
  • Cut sections that don't help shoppers find products, such as long Instagram feeds or multiple announcement bars.

4. Reduce third-party scripts

Chat widgets, review widgets, pop-ups, heatmaps and several ad pixels each slow the store. Keep the ones you actively use, load chat after the page is ready, and remove tracking you no longer look at.

5. Choose a lean theme

Some themes are built for speed; others are packed with features and animations. If your theme is old or heavily modified, moving to a modern, lightweight theme, or a custom theme built for your catalogue, can give the biggest single improvement.

6. Watch your fonts

Use one or two font families, and prefer system fonts or Shopify's font library over loading several custom fonts from different sources.

7. Keep collection pages light

  • Show a sensible number of products per page.
  • Avoid loading a second image for every product just for the hover effect on mobile.
  • Use a fast filter app or Shopify's built-in filters.

Measure again

Re-run your tests after each change. Real-user data in Search Console takes a few weeks to update, so judge immediate results with lab tests and your own phone.

Why it matters

Faster stores generally see more people reach product pages and complete checkout, and you get more from every rupee spent on ads. If your store needs deeper work, our platform comparison and store packages cover rebuilding on a fast, custom theme.
