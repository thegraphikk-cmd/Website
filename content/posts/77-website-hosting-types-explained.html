Hosting is where your website lives: the computer (server) that stores your site and sends it to visitors. The cheapest plan isn't always the best value, and the most expensive isn't always necessary. Here's what the options mean.

Shared hosting

Your site shares a server with hundreds of others.

  • Pros: cheapest, easy control panel, often includes email.
  • Cons: slower and less reliable when other sites on the server get busy; limited resources; quality varies widely between hosts.
  • Good for: small brochure websites with modest traffic.

VPS (virtual private server)

A slice of a server with guaranteed resources.

  • Pros: more power and control, better performance.
  • Cons: needs technical management (updates, security) unless it's a managed VPS.
  • Good for: growing sites, custom applications, WooCommerce stores with more traffic.

Cloud hosting

Your site runs on infrastructure such as AWS, Google Cloud or Azure that can scale up and down.

  • Pros: handles traffic spikes, very reliable, India data-centre options.
  • Cons: pricing can be complex; needs expertise to set up well.
  • Good for: web apps and busy sites with variable traffic.

Managed WordPress hosting

Hosting built for WordPress, with updates, backups, caching and security handled for you.

  • Pros: fast, secure, less maintenance work.
  • Cons: costs more than basic shared hosting.
  • Good for: business WordPress sites that matter to your income.

Hosted platforms (Shopify, Wix, Squarespace)

Hosting is included in the monthly plan; you never manage a server. Good for online stores and simple sites, at the cost of less flexibility. See Shopify pricing and Wix vs WordPress vs custom.

Static and edge hosting

Modern sites built as fast static pages can be served from global networks such as Vercel, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages. These are often very fast, secure and inexpensive for brochure sites, blogs and portfolios, with HTTPS and a CDN built in.

What actually matters when choosing

  • Speed in India: servers or a CDN close to your visitors.
  • Uptime and reliable support.
  • Automatic backups stored off-site; see security and backups.
  • Free SSL that renews automatically.
  • Renewal price, not just the first-year offer.
  • Your access: the account in your name; see who owns your website.

Watch out for

  • "Unlimited" plans that slow down or suspend busy sites.
  • Huge renewal increases after a cheap first year.
  • Hosting your email and website together on a weak plan, so one failure takes down both.

A simple rule of thumb

  • Simple business site: quality shared, managed or static hosting.
  • WordPress site central to your business: managed WordPress hosting.
  • Online store: Shopify, or managed WooCommerce hosting.
  • Custom web app: cloud or VPS with someone managing it.
