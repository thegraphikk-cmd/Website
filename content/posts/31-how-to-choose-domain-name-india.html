Your domain name goes on your visiting cards, your shop board, your Instagram bio and every invoice. Changing it later means reprinting everything and losing some of the Google rankings you've built. So it's worth fifteen minutes of thought before you buy one.

Short answer: pick a name that's short, easy to spell when someone hears it once, and matches your business name. Get the .com if it's free, the .in if it isn't, and register both if you can.

1. Use your business name, not a keyword string

Names like best-web-design-chennai-cheap.com look spammy and don't help rankings the way they did fifteen years ago. Google ranks you on your content, reviews and links, not on keywords in the domain. A brandable name like yourstudio.com builds trust and is easier to remember.

If your exact name is taken, add something natural: your city (yourstudiochennai.com), what you do (yourstudiodesign.com) or a word like "get", "hello" or "try".

2. Pass the phone test

Say the domain out loud to someone over the phone. If you have to spell it, explain a hyphen or say "with two Ls", it will lose you customers. Avoid:

  • Hyphens: people forget them and land on someone else's site.
  • Numbers: "4" or "four"? "2" or "to"?
  • Unusual spellings: kreative, graphix, shoppe. Every odd letter is a chance to mistype.
  • Double letters at word joins: chennaiinteriors.com is easy to misread.

3. Keep it short

Aim for under 15 characters before the extension. Short domains are easier to type on a phone, fit on signage and make cleaner email addresses (info@yourstudio.in beats info@yourcreativedesignstudio.in).

4. Choose the extension

For an Indian business, .com and .in both rank well on Google India. We compare them in detail in .in or .com: which is better for an Indian business? In brief:

  • .com if it's available and you might ever sell outside India.
  • .in if you only serve India or the .com is taken.
  • .co.in works, but people often type .com or .in by mistake.
  • Skip novelty extensions (.xyz, .online, .store) for your main business site. They're cheap in year one, often expensive to renew, and some customers distrust them.

5. Check it isn't someone else's brand

Before you buy, search the name on Google, on the IP India trademark database and on Instagram. A name that's close to an established brand in your industry can get you a legal notice, and it also means your customers will keep finding the other business.

6. Grab the matching social handles

Check Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and LinkedIn for the same name. Matching handles make you easier to find and look more professional. If the exact handle is gone, settle on one consistent variation everywhere.

7. Register it in your own name

This is the mistake we see most. A freelancer or relative registers the domain on their account, then moves on, and the business can't renew, change settings or move hosting. Make sure:

  • The domain is in your business's account, with your email and phone.
  • You have the login, or your developer has delegated access, not ownership.
  • Auto-renew is on, with a card or UPI mandate that won't expire.

8. Buy the obvious variations

If your budget allows, register both .com and .in, and point the second one at your main site. It protects your brand and catches people who type the other version. Common misspellings are only worth buying if your name is genuinely easy to get wrong.

What a domain costs

A .com or .in typically costs a few hundred to around a thousand rupees a year, depending on the registrar and offers. Watch for cheap first-year deals that renew at much higher prices. We break down the full yearly cost in domain and hosting cost in India.

Quick checklist

  • Matches your business name
  • Passes the phone test: no hyphens, numbers or odd spellings
  • Under about 15 characters
  • .com or .in
  • No trademark clash; social handles available
  • Registered in your business's own account, with auto-renew on
