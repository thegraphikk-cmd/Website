Almost every business website collects personal data: names and phone numbers in enquiry forms, addresses at checkout, and visitor data through analytics and ad pixels. India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 sets rules for how this data is handled, with its rules being brought into force in phases. Here's what small business owners should understand.

Note: this is a general overview, not legal advice. Obligations depend on your business and are being phased in. Consult a lawyer for your specific situation, especially if you handle sensitive data, children's data or large volumes.

What counts as personal data

Any information about an identifiable person: name, phone, email, address, IP address, device identifiers, order history, and more. If your website collects any of these, the Act is relevant to you.

Key ideas in plain English

  • Consent: in many cases you need clear, specific and informed consent to process personal data, given through a clear action, not a pre-ticked box.
  • Notice: tell people what data you collect, why, and how they can exercise their rights, in simple language.
  • Purpose limitation: use data only for the purpose you collected it for.
  • Data minimisation: collect only what you need.
  • Security: take reasonable steps to protect data and handle breaches properly.
  • Rights: people can access, correct and erase their data, withdraw consent and raise grievances.
  • Children's data: extra protections, including verifiable parental consent, apply.

What this means for your website

Enquiry and contact forms

  • Ask only for what you need, such as name, phone and message.
  • Add a short line explaining how you'll use the details, with a link to your privacy policy.
  • Use a separate, unticked checkbox if you also want to send marketing messages.

Analytics and ad pixels

Tools like Google Analytics and the Meta Pixel collect visitor data. Explain them in your privacy notice, consider privacy-friendly analytics, and evaluate whether you need a consent banner for tracking. Many businesses now show one, especially when running ads; see Meta ads for small businesses.

Cookie banners

If you use one, make it genuine: clear choices to accept or reject non-essential cookies, no pre-ticked boxes, and settings that actually stop the tracking when declined. A banner that only says "We use cookies, OK" doesn't give real choice.

Online stores

Checkout data, order history and addresses need protecting. Your payment gateway handles card details, but you're responsible for what your store and apps collect. See legal pages for online stores.

Practical steps for a small business

  1. List what you collect: forms, checkout, newsletter, analytics, pixels, chat tools.
  2. Remove what you don't need: old plugins, unused trackers, extra form fields.
  3. Update your privacy policy in plain language, describing what you actually do.
  4. Add clear consent for marketing messages, and for tracking where appropriate.
  5. Name a contact for privacy questions and requests.
  6. Secure your site with HTTPS, updates, strong passwords and limited access; see security and backups.
  7. Don't collect sensitive documents like Aadhaar or bank details through ordinary website forms.

Good privacy is good business

Customers increasingly notice how their data is treated. A site that asks for only what it needs, explains why and keeps it safe builds trust, and is easier to keep compliant as rules evolve. Review your setup at least once a year as part of your maintenance checklist.
