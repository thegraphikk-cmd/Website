Schema markup sounds technical, but the idea is simple: it's a small piece of code that tells Google exactly what's on your page. This is a business, here's its address and opening hours. This is a product, here's its price. Google can then understand your site better, and sometimes show extra details in search results.

What schema does (and doesn't) do

  • Helps Google understand your business, products, articles and FAQs accurately.
  • Can enable rich results: star ratings, prices and stock for products, breadcrumbs, event dates and more.
  • Isn't a magic ranking boost. It helps Google interpret your pages; good content and reviews still do the heavy lifting.

Google decides when to show rich results, and has narrowed some of them over time (FAQ rich results, for example, now appear for only a limited set of sites). It's still worth adding the right markup accurately.

The types that matter for small businesses

LocalBusiness (or a more specific type)

For any business with a location or service area: name, address, phone, opening hours, website, logo and geo-coordinates. Use a specific type where one fits, such as Dentist, Restaurant, HomeAndConstructionBusiness or ProfessionalService.

Organization

Your brand's name, logo, social profiles and contact details, usually on the home page.

Product

For online stores: name, image, price, currency, availability and reviews. This can show price and stock information in search and Google Shopping surfaces.

Article or BlogPosting

For blog posts: headline, author, publish and update dates, and an image.

BreadcrumbList

Shows your page's place in the site (Home › Blog › Post), which can appear in search results instead of a long URL.

FAQPage

For genuine FAQ sections. Even where Google doesn't show FAQ rich results, it helps describe the content clearly.

What it looks like

Most schema is written in a format called JSON-LD, placed in a script tag in the page's code. A simplified example:

{ "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "Dentist", "name": "Smile Dental Care", "telephone": "+91 98XXXXXXXX", "address": { "@type": "PostalAddress", "streetAddress": "12, 2nd Avenue", "addressLocality": "Anna Nagar", "addressRegion": "Tamil Nadu", "postalCode": "600040", "addressCountry": "IN" } }

You don't need to write it by hand if your platform or developer handles it, but the details must match what's visible on your page and your Google Business Profile.

How to add it

  • WordPress: SEO plugins like Yoast or Rank Math add basic schema; check the business details are filled in.
  • Shopify: most themes include product schema; check it's working and complete.
  • Custom sites: your developer adds JSON-LD to the page templates.

How to test it

  1. Use Google's Rich Results Test to check a page.
  2. Use the Schema Markup Validator for a general check.
  3. Watch the Enhancements section in Search Console for errors across your site.

Rules to follow

  • Mark up only what's visible on the page.
  • Don't add fake reviews or ratings to your own business schema.
  • Keep prices, hours and stock accurate.
