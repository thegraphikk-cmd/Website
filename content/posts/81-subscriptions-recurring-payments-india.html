Monthly memberships, tuition fees, meal plans, coffee or skincare boxes, software, donations. Recurring revenue is predictable and valuable. In India, collecting it automatically used to be hard, but UPI AutoPay and card e-mandates now make it practical for small businesses.

Your options

1. UPI AutoPay

The customer approves a mandate once in their UPI app (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm and others), and payments are debited automatically on schedule. It's the most popular option for Indian subscribers.

2. Card mandates

Customers save a credit or debit card for recurring charges, with a registered e-mandate. Works well for software and higher-value plans.

3. eNACH (bank account mandates)

Debits directly from a bank account. Common for loans, insurance and larger recurring amounts.

4. Payment links each cycle

The simplest option: send a payment link or UPI request every month. No mandate needed, but more manual work and more missed payments.

RBI rules, in brief

Recurring payments in India follow RBI's e-mandate framework. In practice:

  • Customers authorise the mandate with additional authentication when setting it up.
  • They receive a pre-debit notification before each charge, and can cancel the mandate.
  • Payments above a set limit need extra authentication each time. The limit has been ₹15,000 for most categories, with higher limits for some, such as insurance and mutual funds.

Your payment gateway handles most of this, but rules get updated, so check the current limits with your provider.

Gateways that support subscriptions

Several Indian payment gateways, including Razorpay, Cashfree and PayU, offer subscription or recurring payment products with UPI AutoPay and card mandates. Designing a good subscription offer

  • Clear value: what's included each month, and why subscribing beats buying once.
  • Simple plans: two or three options, monthly and yearly with a discount.
  • Flexibility: pause, skip or change plan easily. People subscribe more readily when they know they can stop.
  • Honest cancellation: make it easy. Hard-to-cancel subscriptions create chargebacks and bad reviews.
  • Reminders before renewals, especially yearly ones.

Handle failed payments

Some recurring payments will fail: insufficient balance, expired cards or revoked mandates. Set up automatic retries, notify customers by WhatsApp or email with a payment link, and give a short grace period before pausing the service.

  • Consumables: coffee, tea, pet food, skincare, groceries.

Show the terms clearly

On your pricing and checkout pages, state the amount, billing frequency, renewal date and how to cancel. Clear terms build trust and reduce disputes; see policy pages for online stores.
