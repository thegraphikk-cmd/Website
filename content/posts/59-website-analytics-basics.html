Analytics tools can show hundreds of charts, which is why many business owners never look at them. You don't need most of them. Five numbers tell you whether your website is working and what to do next.

Which tool to use

  • Google Analytics 4: free and powerful, but complex. Good if you run ads or want detailed reports.
  • Simpler, privacy-friendly tools such as Vercel Analytics, Plausible or similar: cleaner dashboards with the basics.
  • Shopify Analytics: built in for online stores, with sales and conversion data.
  • Google Search Console: for what happens on Google before people reach your site. See Search Console for beginners.

Pick one main tool and check it monthly. Consistency beats complexity.

1. Visitors

How many people visit your site each month. Look at the trend over months, not daily ups and downs. A steady rise means your marketing and SEO are working. A sudden drop is worth investigating: a Google update, a broken page or a lost listing.

2. Traffic sources

Where visitors come from:

  • Organic search: Google and Bing results.
  • Direct: typed your address, or clicked from WhatsApp and apps that don't pass referrer data.
  • Social: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn.
  • Referral: links from other websites and directories.
  • Paid: Google or Meta ads.

This tells you which channels deserve more effort. If almost everything is direct, your site isn't being discovered yet; see SEO for a new website.

3. Top pages

Which pages get the most visits. You'll often find a blog post or a service page doing far better than expected. Make sure those pages have a clear next step, like a button, form or WhatsApp link, and link to them from elsewhere on your site.

4. Enquiries (conversions)

The number that matters most: how many people contacted you or bought something. Track:

  • Form submissions
  • WhatsApp button clicks
  • Phone number taps
  • Purchases, for online stores

These usually need a little setup, such as event tracking for button clicks. It's worth doing once, properly.

5. Conversion rate

Enquiries divided by visitors. If 1,000 people visit and 20 enquire, that's 2%. Typical rates vary widely by industry and traffic source, but the useful comparison is with yourself over time. If visitors are rising but conversion rate is falling, the site may need clearer content or a simpler path to contact you; see writing content that converts.

Numbers you can mostly ignore

  • Bounce rate on its own: someone who reads your contact page and calls you "bounced", but that's a success.
  • Daily fluctuations: look at weeks and months.
  • Vanity totals: page views without enquiries don't pay bills.

A 15-minute monthly review

  1. Compare visitors and enquiries with last month and the same month last year.
  2. Check which sources grew or shrank.
  3. Look at your top 10 pages and make sure each has a clear call to action.
  4. Pick one improvement for the next month.

Small, regular improvements based on real numbers are how good websites get better.
