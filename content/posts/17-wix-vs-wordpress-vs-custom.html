There are three common ways to get a business website: build it yourself on a website builder like Wix, use WordPress, or have a developer build a custom site. Each suits a different kind of business. Here's a straightforward comparison to help you choose.

Website builders like Wix

Good for: very small businesses that want to launch quickly, on a small budget, and are happy to build it themselves.

  • Drag-and-drop editing with no technical knowledge needed
  • Hosting, security and updates included
  • Templates look decent with little effort

Watch out for: monthly fees that continue forever, limits on what you can customise, and the difficulty of moving the site elsewhere later. Builder sites can rank on Google, but heavier templates often load slowly on mobile.

WordPress

Good for: businesses that want to publish content regularly, need lots of features through plugins, or want a site they can grow.

  • Powers a large share of the web, so developers are easy to find
  • Thousands of themes and plugins for almost any feature
  • Strong for blogging and content-led SEO
  • You own the site and can move hosting any time

Watch out for: WordPress needs regular updates and good hosting. Sites built with heavy page builders and dozens of plugins become slow and vulnerable. A neglected WordPress site is one of the most common targets for hacking.

Custom-built websites

Good for: businesses where design, speed and conversion matter, and those with specific needs that templates don't handle well.

  • Designed around your brand and customers, not a template
  • Can be extremely fast because there's no unused code
  • Static custom sites need very little maintenance and are hard to hack
  • Easy to add exactly the features you need

Watch out for: higher upfront cost, and making sure content editing is set up, so you aren't dependent on a developer for every text change.

How to decide

  • Testing an idea on a very small budget? A website builder is fine for now.
  • Planning to publish lots of articles and manage content yourself? WordPress, properly built and maintained.
  • Want a fast, distinctive site that brings in leads, with low maintenance? Custom-built.
  • Selling products? Look at Shopify or WooCommerce instead; read our comparison.

Think three years ahead

The cheapest option today isn't always the cheapest over three years. Add up subscription fees, plugin licences, maintenance and the cost of rebuilding when you outgrow the platform. For a business site that needs to bring in customers for years, a well-built site you fully own is usually the better investment.
