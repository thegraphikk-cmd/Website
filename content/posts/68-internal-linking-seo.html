Internal links are simply links from one page on your website to another. They cost nothing, take minutes to add, and help both visitors and Google. Yet many small business sites have pages that nothing links to, so Google rarely visits them and they almost never rank.

Why internal links matter

  • Discovery: Google finds pages by following links. Pages with no internal links ("orphan pages") may be crawled rarely or not indexed.
  • Importance: pages that many other pages link to look more important.
  • Context: the link text tells Google what the target page is about.
  • Visitors: links guide readers from a blog post to the service that solves their problem.

If Search Console shows pages as "Discovered – currently not indexed", weak internal linking is often part of the reason. See Search Console for beginners.

The basic structure

  • Home page links to your main service pages.
  • Service pages link to related services, relevant blog posts and the contact page.
  • Blog posts link to the service page that matches the topic, and to two or three related posts.
  • Every page links back up through the menu and footer.

Think of it as a web: your most important pages should be reachable within two or three clicks from the home page.

Use descriptive link text

The clickable words matter:

  • Weak: "Click here", "Read more", "this page".
  • Better: "how much a website costs in India", "our bridal makeup packages".

Keep it natural. You don't need the exact keyword every time; varied, descriptive text is fine.

A 30-minute internal linking session

  1. List your five most important pages (usually main services).
  2. For each, find three to five existing pages or posts that mention that topic.
  3. Add a link in the body text where it fits naturally.
  4. Check new blog posts link to at least one service page and one related post.
  5. Make sure no important page is missing from the menu or a hub page.

Hub pages help

A hub page collects links to everything on one topic, such as "Areas we serve", "All our services" or a blog category page. They're helpful for visitors and give Google a clear map of your site. See service area pages for an example.

Common mistakes

  • New blog posts that nothing links to.
  • Links inside images or buttons with no text, which give no context.
  • Broken internal links after changing page addresses; see fixing broken links and 404 errors.
  • Hundreds of links stuffed into a footer.

When you publish something new

Go back to two or three older, related pages and add a link to the new one. It's the fastest way to get a new page discovered and ranked. Combined with keyword research, it's one of the highest-value SEO habits for a small site.
