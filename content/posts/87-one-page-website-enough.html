A one-page website puts everything on a single scrolling page: who you are, what you offer, proof and how to contact you. It's quick to build and easy to update. For some businesses it's the perfect start; for others it quietly limits how many customers find them.

When a one-page website works well

  • A single main service or product: a consultant, a photographer, a specific event.
  • A new business that needs a credible online presence quickly.
  • Most customers come from referrals, Instagram or offline, not Google search.
  • Campaigns and launches: a landing page for an event, product or ad campaign; see landing page vs website.
  • A tight budget, with plans to expand later.

When it holds you back

  • Several distinct services: one page can't rank well for "root canal", "braces" and "teeth whitening" all at once.
  • Several locations you want to be found in.
  • Search is important: Google ranks pages, and a single page can only target a limited set of searches.
  • You want to publish content: guides, case studies and a blog need their own pages.
  • Lots of information: very long single pages get slow and hard to navigate.

What a good one-page site includes

  1. Hero section: a clear headline saying what you do, for whom and where, with one main button.
  2. Services: short descriptions of what you offer, with prices or "starting from".
  3. Why choose you: three or four specific reasons.
  4. Proof: reviews, portfolio images, client logos or numbers.
  5. How it works: three to five simple steps.
  6. FAQs: the questions customers ask most.
  7. Contact: form, WhatsApp, phone, address and map.

Add a sticky menu that jumps to each section, and repeat the main button after key sections.

SEO tips for a one-page site

  • Focus the page title and main heading on your core service and location.
  • Use clear section headings with the terms customers search.
  • Keep it fast: compress images and avoid heavy animations.
  • Set up your Google Business Profile and link it to the site. For local businesses, it often matters more than the page count.

Plan to grow

Build it so you can add pages later: a page per main service, an about page, a blog. Choose a platform and structure that makes this easy, so you don't have to start over. Many businesses start with one page and expand within a year as they learn what customers search for.

The bottom line

A well-made one-page website beats a sprawling, outdated multi-page one. But if you offer several services or rely on Google to bring new customers, plan for separate pages sooner rather than later. For costs, see how much a website costs in India.
