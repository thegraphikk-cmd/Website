A website doesn't have an expiry date, but it does age. Technology moves on, your business grows, and customer expectations change. Here are seven signs it's time for a redesign, and a few cases where smaller fixes are enough.

1. It's awkward on a phone

Most of your visitors are on mobile. If people have to pinch and zoom, menus are hard to tap, or text runs off the screen, you're losing them. Open your site on your own phone and try to find your prices and contact details in under ten seconds.

2. It's slow

If pages take more than about three seconds to load on mobile data, many visitors leave before they see anything. Old themes, heavy sliders, too many plugins and huge images are the usual causes. Test your speed: if it's poor across every page, a rebuild often fixes it more cheaply than patching.

3. Enquiries have dropped

If traffic is steady but calls, forms and WhatsApp messages are falling, the site may not be convincing people anymore. Common reasons: an unclear message, no prices, buried contact details or outdated photos. See writing content that converts.

4. The content is out of date

  • Services you no longer offer, or new ones missing.
  • Old prices, old team photos, an old address.
  • A blog or news section last updated years ago.
  • A footer showing "© 2019".

Outdated content quietly tells visitors you might not be active, or not paying attention.

5. You can't update it yourself

If changing a phone number or adding a photo means calling a developer and waiting a week, the site will always fall behind. A modern site should let you edit everyday content easily, or come with a maintenance arrangement that makes changes quick.

6. Security and technical problems

  • A "Not secure" warning in the browser; see how to fix it.
  • An old WordPress version or abandoned plugins that can't be updated.
  • Past hacks, spam injections or unexplained redirects.
  • Broken forms you only discover when a customer complains.

7. Your business has outgrown it

Maybe you've rebranded, moved upmarket, added an online store or expanded to new cities. If the website still reflects the business you were five years ago, it's sending the wrong signal to the customers you want now.

When a redesign isn't needed

Sometimes a few targeted fixes are enough:

  • Slow, but otherwise fine: compress images and remove heavy plugins.
  • Outdated text: rewrite key pages and update photos.
  • Missing pages: add service pages or a blog.
  • Not getting found: start with Search Console and local SEO before redesigning.

Redesign without losing what works

A redesign can hurt Google rankings if old page addresses disappear without redirects, or content that ranked well is cut. Plan redirects, keep valuable content and check Search Console after launch. Our guide to redesigning without losing your rankings covers it step by step.
