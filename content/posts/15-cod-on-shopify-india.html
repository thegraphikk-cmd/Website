Cash on delivery is still a big part of online shopping in India, especially outside the big metros and for first-time buyers who don't yet trust a brand. But COD orders cost more to fulfil, and undelivered or refused COD orders (RTO, or return to origin) can quietly eat your margin. Here's how to offer COD without letting it hurt your business.

Turning on COD in Shopify

In Settings → Payments, under manual payment methods, add Cash on Delivery (COD). Add clear instructions customers will see at checkout, for example "Pay in cash or UPI to the delivery partner."

Your shipping partner must support COD for the pincodes you deliver to, and will remit collected cash to you after delivery, minus their fees.

The real cost of COD

  • COD handling fee from your courier or aggregator, usually a flat amount or a percentage of the order value.
  • Delayed cash flow, since COD money reaches you days after delivery.
  • RTO: if the customer refuses or isn't reachable, you pay forward shipping and return shipping, and the product may come back damaged.

For some categories, especially low-priced fashion and impulse buys, RTO rates on COD can be much higher than on prepaid orders.

Adding a COD charge

Shopify doesn't add a COD fee natively in a flexible way. Common approaches are:

  • A COD fee app that adds a charge when COD is selected.
  • Shipping rules that charge shipping on lower-value orders while prepaid orders get free shipping.

Keep the fee modest and explain it clearly. A surprise charge at checkout increases abandoned carts.

Limiting COD sensibly

  • By order value: disable COD above a certain amount, where a refused order would hurt most, or below a minimum where the fee isn't worth it.
  • By pincode: turn off COD for areas with consistently high RTO or where your courier doesn't support it.
  • By product: exclude personalised or made-to-order items from COD.
  • By customer history: some apps can restrict COD for customers with previous refusals.

Nudge customers to pay online

  • Offer a small prepaid discount or free shipping on prepaid orders.
  • Make UPI the most visible payment option; many customers choose COD only because they don't trust card payments.
  • Show trust signals near checkout: reviews, return policy, contact number and secure payment badges.

Reduce RTO on COD orders

  1. Confirm COD orders on WhatsApp before dispatch. An automated confirmation message with "Confirm" and "Cancel" buttons filters out impulse and fake orders.
  2. Validate addresses and phone numbers at checkout to catch incomplete details.
  3. Dispatch fast. The longer an order takes to arrive, the more likely the customer has changed their mind.
  4. Send tracking updates on WhatsApp or SMS so customers expect the delivery.
  5. Offer conversion to prepaid with a payment link and a small incentive after the order is placed.
  6. Track RTO by pincode and product each month, and adjust COD rules based on the data.

Should you offer COD at all?

For most new D2C brands in India, yes. Removing COD entirely can cut conversions significantly, especially for new customers. The goal isn't to eliminate COD but to make it profitable: charge for it fairly, limit it where it's risky, and steadily move customers to prepaid.
