A new website doesn't appear on Google's first page the day it goes live. Google has to find it, read it and learn to trust it. The good news: the first 90 days decide how quickly that happens, and most of the work is straightforward.

Short answer: get indexed in week one, nail your Google Business Profile and service pages in month one, then publish helpful content and earn a few genuine links. Expect early local results in 1 to 3 months and stronger rankings in 4 to 12 months.

Days 1–7: get found

  • Set up Google Search Console, verify your domain and submit your sitemap. Our Search Console guide walks through it.
  • Request indexing for the home page and main service pages.
  • Check nothing blocks Google: no "noindex" left over from development, and robots.txt allows crawling.
  • Set up analytics so you can see visits and enquiries from day one.
  • Link your website from your Google Business Profile, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and WhatsApp Business.

Days 8–30: the foundations

Google Business Profile

For local businesses this often brings enquiries before your website ranks at all. Complete every field, add real photos, choose the right categories and start collecting reviews. See adding your business to Google Maps and getting more reviews.

One page per service

A single "Services" page listing everything rarely ranks. Give each main service its own page with a clear title, like "Root Canal Treatment in Anna Nagar, Chennai", and a genuinely useful description: what it is, who it's for, process, price range and FAQs.

Titles and descriptions

Every page needs a unique title (around 50 to 60 characters) with the service and location, and a description that makes people want to click.

Speed and mobile

Most of your visitors are on phones. A slow site ranks and converts worse; see Core Web Vitals.

Days 31–60: content that answers questions

Think about the questions customers ask before buying: prices, timelines, comparisons, "is it worth it", "how to choose". Each is a potential blog post or FAQ. A post that answers one question well beats a vague "Top 10 tips" article.

  • Aim for one or two helpful posts a week if you can.
  • Link each post to the relevant service page, and service pages to related posts.
  • Use the words customers use, not industry jargon.

Days 61–90: trust and links

Google trusts sites that other sites mention. For a small business, the easy, safe wins are:

  • Business listings with consistent name, address and phone; see free listings that help local businesses.
  • Suppliers, partners and associations that can list you on their site.
  • Local press or community sites when you do something newsworthy.
  • Clients who can credit or mention you.

Avoid buying links or "1000 backlinks for ₹999" offers. They don't help and can hurt.

What to measure

  • Impressions in Search Console should rise first, often within weeks.
  • Average position improves next, as Google tests your pages.
  • Clicks and enquiries follow once pages reach the first page.

If impressions are growing but clicks aren't, improve your titles. If nothing is moving after two months, check indexing first.

How long does SEO really take?

For a local service business in a moderately competitive area, it's common to see Google Maps enquiries within a month or two and organic rankings for service pages within three to six months. Competitive city-wide terms can take a year. Consistency matters more than any single trick.
