Why prices vary so much

Template vs custom design

A template-based site reuses a ready design and adjusts colours, fonts and content. A custom site is designed from scratch around your brand and customers. Custom costs more but usually looks more distinctive and converts better.

Number of unique page layouts

Ten service pages that share one layout cost far less than ten pages that each need their own design.

Who writes the content

If you supply finished text and photos, the project is cheaper and faster. If the designer writes copy or arranges photography, expect to pay for that time.

Features

Booking systems, payment gateways, multi-language pages, CRM integrations and member logins all add development time.

SEO and speed work

A site that is technically set up to rank (fast loading, proper headings, schema, sitemap, Search Console) takes more care than one that just looks good.

Freelancer vs agency prices in Chennai

Freelancers in Chennai typically charge less than agencies because they have no office or team overheads. Agencies charge more but usually provide design, development, testing and project management under one roof. A small studio sits in between: lower overheads than a large agency, more structure than a solo freelancer.

Very low prices, such as a full business website for ₹5,000, usually mean a lightly edited template, no SEO setup and little support after launch. That can be fine for testing an idea, but often has to be redone within a year.

What should be included in the price

Before you compare quotes, check whether each one includes:

  • Mobile-friendly design tested on real phones
  • Basic on-page SEO: titles, descriptions, headings, sitemap
  • Contact form and WhatsApp button
  • Google Business Profile link and map
  • SSL certificate (the padlock)
  • Number of revision rounds
  • Training so you can edit content yourself
  • Support period after launch
  • Ownership of the domain, hosting and files in your name

A cheaper quote that leaves out half of these isn't really cheaper.

How to keep the cost down

  1. Start with the pages that bring in customers, and add the rest later.
  2. Prepare your text, photos and logo before the project starts.
  3. Use a quality template for the first version if budget is tight, then invest in custom design once the site is earning.
  4. Choose features you will actually use in the first six months.
