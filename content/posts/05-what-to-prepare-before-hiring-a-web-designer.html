Most website projects that run late don't stall on design or code. They stall waiting for content and decisions from the business owner. Preparing a few things before you hire anyone will get you a more accurate quote, a faster build and a site that does what you need.

1. Your goal, in one sentence

Decide what the website must achieve. "Get 20 enquiries a month for interior design projects in Chennai" is a goal. "Look professional" is not. The goal shapes every decision: which pages you need, what goes at the top of the home page, and how people contact you.

2. Who your customers are

Write down who buys from you, what they worry about before buying, and the questions they ask most often. Good websites answer those questions before the customer has to message you.

3. A page list

List the pages you think you need. A typical small business site has:

  • Home
  • Services or products (one page per main service is better for Google)
  • Work, gallery or case studies
  • About
  • Contact
  • Privacy policy and terms

Your designer will refine it, but a starting list makes quotes comparable.

4. Content: text and photos

This is the biggest bottleneck. Gather:

  • A short description of each service, with starting prices if you can share them
  • Your business story for the About page
  • Real photos of your work, team and premises (real photos beat stock images)
  • Customer reviews or testimonials you have permission to use
  • Answers to your ten most common customer questions

Rough notes are fine if your designer offers copywriting. Just make sure the facts are there.

5. Branding

Send your logo in the best format you have (ideally SVG or a high-resolution PNG), plus brand colours and fonts if you have them. If you don't have a logo yet, sort that out first or ask whether the designer can help.

6. Websites you like, and why

Share three to five websites you like, even from other industries, and note what you like about each: the layout, the colours, how easy it is to find prices. Also share any you dislike. This saves rounds of revisions.

7. Features you actually need

List must-have features and nice-to-haves separately:

  • WhatsApp chat button and enquiry form
  • Online booking or appointments
  • Online payments (UPI, cards)
  • Product catalogue or store
  • Blog
  • Tamil and English versions

Every feature adds cost, so be honest about what you will use in the first six months.

8. Domain, hosting and logins

If you already own a domain or have an old website, find the login details for your domain registrar, hosting and any existing website admin. Make sure the domain is registered in your name or your company's, not a previous designer's.

9. Budget and deadline

Share a realistic budget range and any fixed date such as a launch, event or ad campaign. A good designer will tell you what fits the budget rather than quoting everything at the maximum.

10. Who decides

Decide who gives feedback and final approval. Projects slow down when feedback comes from several people with different opinions. One decision-maker, consulting others, works best.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ One-sentence goal
  • ☐ Customer description and common questions
  • ☐ Page list
  • ☐ Text for each page (or notes)
  • ☐ Photos and logo
  • ☐ 3–5 reference websites
  • ☐ Feature list: must-have vs later
  • ☐ Domain and hosting logins
  • ☐ Budget range and deadline
  • ☐ One decision-maker
