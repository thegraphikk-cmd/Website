If Chrome shows "Not secure" next to your website's address, visitors notice. Many will leave before reading anything, and those who stay are less likely to fill in a form or pay. The fix is usually simple and often free.

What "Not secure" means

It means your page is loading over HTTP instead of HTTPS. With HTTP, anything a visitor types, such as their name, phone number or card details, travels unencrypted. Browsers warn about this to protect users.

HTTPS needs an SSL certificate (technically TLS, but everyone still says SSL) installed on your website's server.

Why it matters

  • Trust: a warning next to your name makes your business look careless or unsafe.
  • Enquiries: people hesitate to fill in forms on "Not secure" pages.
  • Google: HTTPS is a ranking signal, and Chrome highlights insecure pages more strongly than it used to.
  • Payments: payment gateways and many features simply don't work without HTTPS.

The four common causes

1. No SSL certificate at all

Older sites, or sites on cheap hosting set up years ago, may never have had one.

2. The certificate expired

Paid certificates last a year or so and must be renewed. If nobody renews it, visitors see a full-page warning, which is worse than "Not secure".

3. HTTPS exists, but HTTP still works

If http://yourbusiness.in doesn't redirect to https://, people following old links or typing the address see the insecure version.

4. Mixed content

The page loads over HTTPS, but some images, scripts or fonts still load from http:// addresses. Browsers may block them or show a warning.

How to fix it

Step 1: Get a certificate

Most good hosts include a free certificate from Let's Encrypt, which renews automatically. Look for "SSL" or "Let's Encrypt" in your hosting panel and switch it on. Platforms like Shopify, Wix and Vercel include SSL automatically. There's rarely a reason to pay for a basic certificate for a small business site.

Step 2: Redirect HTTP to HTTPS

Turn on "Force HTTPS" in your hosting panel, or ask your developer to add a permanent (301) redirect so every HTTP address goes to its HTTPS version.

Step 3: Fix mixed content

Open your site in Chrome, right-click, choose Inspect and look at the Console for "Mixed Content" warnings. Update those links to https. On WordPress, a search-and-replace of http://yourdomain with https://yourdomain in the database usually fixes most of them.

Step 4: Update the rest

  • Make sure your Google Search Console property covers the HTTPS version.
  • Update your website link on your Google Business Profile, Instagram and other profiles.
  • Check your sitemap lists https addresses.

How to check it's working

  • Type http://yourdomain in a browser. It should switch to https:// automatically.
  • The padlock (or settings icon) should appear with no warning.
  • Check a few inner pages and your contact form, not just the home page.

Prevent it happening again

Use a host that renews certificates automatically, and make sure someone gets the expiry emails. This is one of the small jobs covered by ongoing maintenance; see website maintenance cost in India and our guide to website security and backups.
