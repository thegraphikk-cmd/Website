Small business websites get hacked far more often than owners expect, usually not by someone targeting you, but by automated bots scanning thousands of sites for old software and weak passwords. The result can be spam pages, redirects to scam sites, a Google warning or a site that simply disappears. Most of it is preventable with a few habits.

1. Backups: your safety net

If everything else fails, a recent backup lets you restore your site in hours instead of rebuilding it.

  • Automatic: daily or weekly, depending on how often the site changes.
  • Off-site: stored somewhere other than your web server, such as cloud storage.
  • Complete: files and database, for WordPress and similar platforms.
  • Several versions: keep at least a few weeks, in case a problem goes unnoticed.
  • Tested: restore a backup at least once so you know it works.

2. Keep everything updated

Outdated software is the most common way sites get hacked. On WordPress, that means core, themes and plugins. Update regularly, and remove plugins and themes you don't use. Abandoned plugins that haven't been updated in years are a real risk. Hosted platforms like Shopify handle platform updates for you, but you should still review the apps you install.

3. Strong logins

  • Unique, long passwords for your website, hosting, domain and email, kept in a password manager.
  • Two-factor authentication wherever it's offered, especially for domain and hosting accounts.
  • Separate logins for each person who needs access, with only the permissions they need.
  • Remove access for staff, freelancers and agencies who no longer work with you.

4. Own your accounts

Your domain, hosting and website admin should be registered to your business, with your email and phone. If a former developer controls them, you can be locked out of your own site. See how to register a domain properly.

5. Choose decent hosting

Very cheap shared hosting often means outdated server software and no help when things go wrong. Look for hosts with automatic SSL, malware scanning, backups and responsive support. Make sure HTTPS is on everywhere; see fixing "Not secure" warnings.

6. Protect forms and logins

  • Add spam protection to contact forms (a CAPTCHA or a hidden spam trap).
  • Limit login attempts and change default admin usernames.
  • Never collect sensitive documents like Aadhaar or bank details through a basic website form.

Signs your site may be hacked

  • Google shows "This site may be hacked" or a red warning screen.
  • Strange pages in Google results for your site (pharma, casino or foreign-language spam).
  • Visitors on mobile get redirected to other sites.
  • Your host suspends the account or emails about malware.
  • Search Console reports a security issue.

If you're hacked

  1. Don't panic, and don't delete everything.
  2. Change all passwords: website, hosting, domain, email and FTP.
  3. Restore a clean backup from before the hack, or have a professional clean the site.
  4. Update everything and remove unknown users, plugins and files.
  5. Check Search Console's Security Issues report, fix what it lists and request a review.
  6. Find out how it happened, so it doesn't happen again.

Make it someone's job

Security fails when everyone assumes someone else is handling it. Either set a monthly reminder to check backups and updates yourself, or include it in a maintenance plan. See website maintenance cost in India for what that typically covers.
