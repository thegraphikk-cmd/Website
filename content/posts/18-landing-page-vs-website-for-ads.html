You're paying for every click from Google Ads or Meta ads. Where those clicks land decides whether that money turns into enquiries or disappears. Many small businesses send ad traffic to their home page and wonder why the leads don't come. Here's the difference between a website and a landing page, and when you need each.

The difference

A website is built for exploration. It has navigation, several services, an About page and a blog, which is great for people researching you.

A landing page is built for one action. It focuses on a single offer, such as "Book a free consultation" or "Get a quote for a modular kitchen", and removes everything that distracts from it, often including the main navigation.

Why the home page usually underperforms for ads

  • The ad promised one thing; the home page talks about everything.
  • Visitors have to hunt for the relevant information.
  • Menus and links give them many ways to wander off.
  • It's harder to measure which ad produced which lead.

Someone who clicked an ad for "bridal makeup package Chennai" should land on a page about bridal makeup packages, with prices, photos, reviews and a WhatsApp button, not on a general salon home page.

When sending ads to your website is fine

  • Brand searches, where people search your business name
  • Ads for a specific service that already has a strong, focused page on your site
  • Online stores sending people to specific product or collection pages

What a high-converting landing page includes

  1. A headline that matches the ad. Same offer, same words.
  2. One clear call to action, repeated: WhatsApp, call, or a short form.
  3. The key details: what's included, price or starting price, area served, timelines.
  4. Proof: reviews, photos of real work, client logos, numbers.
  5. Answers to objections: a short FAQ on price, process and guarantees.
  6. Fast loading on mobile. Ad visitors are impatient, and Google Ads considers landing page experience in Quality Score.
  7. Tracking: Google Ads conversion tracking and the Meta Pixel set up correctly, so you know which ads produce leads.

What about Meta lead forms?

Meta's instant forms let people submit details without leaving Instagram or Facebook. They can produce cheap leads, but those leads are often lower quality because submitting is so easy. A landing page asks a bit more effort and usually brings fewer but more serious enquiries. Test both and compare the cost per customer, not just the cost per lead.

One landing page or several?

Create a landing page for each distinct offer or audience. If you advertise both kitchen interiors and full-home interiors, they deserve separate pages with different photos, prices and FAQs. Start with your best-selling service and add more once the first is working.

Do you still need a website?

Yes. People who see your ads often search your name before contacting you, and they expect a proper website. The landing page converts ad traffic; the website builds trust and brings in free traffic from Google. See why a website still matters even if you're active on social media.
