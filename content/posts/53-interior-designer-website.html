For interior designers and architects, the website is the portfolio clients check before they call, often after seeing your work on Instagram. A strong site makes your work look as good as it is and filters in the right clients. A weak one hides great projects behind slow galleries and vague text.

Project pages, not just a gallery

A grid of 200 photos tells clients you're busy, not that you're right for them. Give each key project its own page, like a short case study:

  • Project name, type and location: "3BHK apartment, OMR, Chennai".
  • The brief: what the client wanted and any challenges (small space, budget, a joint family, Vastu).
  • Your approach: key design decisions, materials and colours.
  • Before and after photos where possible.
  • Scope and timeline: full home, kitchen and wardrobes, turnkey, and how long it took.
  • A client quote, with permission.

Ten well-explained projects win more work than a hundred unlabelled photos, and each project page can rank in search for its location and style.

Photography makes or breaks it

  • Invest in professional photos of your best projects; it pays back quickly.
  • Shoot in daylight, with the space tidied and styled.
  • Include wide shots for context and details for craftsmanship: joinery, textures, lighting.
  • Label 3D renders clearly so clients know what's built and what's proposed.

Large photos must still load fast. Compress and serve modern formats; see how to test your website speed.

Explain how you work

Clients worry about cost overruns, delays and endless site visits. A simple process page reduces that anxiety:

  1. Consultation and site visit
  2. Concept and mood boards
  3. 3D designs and approvals
  4. Quotation and agreement
  5. Execution with timelines and updates
  6. Handover

Talk about budgets

You don't need a full price list, but indicating typical budgets ("Full 2BHK interiors typically from ₹X lakh") or design fees helps clients self-select. You'll spend less time on enquiries that were never a fit.

Make enquiring easy

  • A short form: project type, location, approximate budget, timeline, phone.
  • A WhatsApp button, since many clients prefer to send photos of their space.
  • Your studio address with map, if clients can visit.

Connect Instagram and the website

Instagram is great for discovery; your website is where clients go to decide. Link project posts to their project pages, and embed a small Instagram feed on the site. We explain the balance in do I need a website if I have Instagram?

Be found locally

  • Set up your Google Business Profile with project photos.
  • Use locations in project titles and page titles: "Interior Designer in Anna Nagar, Chennai".
  • Collect Google reviews after each handover.
