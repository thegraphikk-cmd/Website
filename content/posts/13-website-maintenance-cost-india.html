A website isn't a one-time purchase in the way a printed brochure is. Domains renew, software needs updates, and things occasionally break. But many small businesses in India are either paying too much for maintenance they don't need, or paying nothing and letting the site slowly fall apart. Here's what maintenance actually involves and what it typically costs.

What website maintenance includes

Fixed yearly costs

  • Domain renewal: roughly ₹500–₹1,500 a year.
  • Hosting: ₹2,000–₹10,000 a year for a typical small business site; more for busy stores.
  • SSL certificate: often free with hosting today.
  • Paid themes or plugins: if your site uses premium WordPress plugins, they usually need yearly licences for updates.
  • Business email: if you use Google Workspace or similar, charged per user.

Technical upkeep

  • Software, theme and plugin updates (important for WordPress sites)
  • Backups and the ability to restore them
  • Security monitoring and malware cleanup if needed
  • Uptime monitoring
  • Fixing broken forms, links or layouts after updates

Content and growth (often billed separately)

  • Updating prices, services, team and photos
  • Adding blog posts or new pages
  • SEO work and speed improvements

Monthly retainers that include content updates and SEO work are a different service and can cost much more. Make sure you know which one you're paying for.

Why the type of site matters so much

WordPress and WooCommerce sites run software that needs regular updates. Skip them for a year and you risk a hacked or broken site. They need real maintenance.

Static sites and hosted platforms (like Shopify, or a well-built static site) have far less to maintain because the platform handles security and servers. You mainly pay for content changes.

When choosing how your site is built, ask what it will cost to keep it running for three years, not just to launch it.

Do you need a monthly maintenance plan?

You probably do if:

  • Your site runs WordPress or WooCommerce with several plugins
  • You take payments or bookings and downtime costs you money
  • You change content weekly and don't want to do it yourself

You probably don't if your site is simple, rarely changes, and runs on a platform that handles updates. Pay for changes when you need them instead.

Questions to ask before signing a maintenance contract

  1. What exactly is included each month? Updates, backups, content changes, hours?
  2. How often are backups taken, and where are they stored?
  3. How quickly do you respond if the site goes down?
  4. What happens to unused hours?
  5. If I stop the plan, do I keep full access to my site, hosting and domain?

How to keep maintenance costs low

  • Use as few plugins and apps as possible.
  • Keep your domain and hosting in your own name, paid yearly.
  • Learn to make simple text and photo changes yourself.
  • Batch content updates instead of requesting them one at a time.
