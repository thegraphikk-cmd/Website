It happens more often than you'd think. A business pays for a website, the developer moves on or a dispute starts, and suddenly the owner can't change a phone number, renew the domain or move hosting. Before or after you build a site, it's worth checking that you actually control it.

The five things to own

1. The domain name

The most important one. Whoever controls the domain controls where your website and email point. It should be registered in your business's name, in an account you can log into. See how to register a domain properly.

2. Hosting

The server where your website lives. Ideally the hosting account is in your name too, or your agency hosts it under a written agreement that lets you move it whenever you want.

3. Website admin access

An administrator login for WordPress, Shopify or whatever platform you use, in your name, not a shared login.

4. Content

Your text, photos, logo and videos. Content you provide stays yours. Content the agency creates for you (copy, photos, graphics) should be assigned to you on payment. Stock photos are licensed, not owned; make sure the licence covers your use.

5. Design and code

For custom websites, agree whether you get the full rights to the design and code, or a licence to use it. Themes and plugins are licensed from their makers either way.

Other accounts to keep in your name

  • Google Business Profile, Search Console and Analytics.
  • Business email (Google Workspace or Zoho).
  • Payment gateway and shipping accounts for online stores.
  • Social media pages.

Give your agency or freelancer access as a user or manager, rather than letting them own these accounts.

Put it in writing

Your website agreement should say clearly:

  • Who owns the domain, content, design and code after final payment.
  • That you'll receive all logins at handover.
  • What happens to hosting and maintenance if you stop working together.
  • That they'll help you move the site if you leave, and at what cost.

More on this in what to include in a website design contract.

Check your current website today

  1. Look up your domain's registrar and try logging in. Do you know the account email?
  2. Check you can log in to hosting and the website admin.
  3. Confirm renewal dates and that auto-renew is on with a working payment method.
  4. Store all logins in a password manager the business controls.

If someone else controls your domain

  • Ask politely first for the domain to be transferred to your account. Most registrars allow a free or cheap "push" to another account.
  • Collect proof you're the business owner: invoices for the website, GST certificate, emails.
  • Contact the registrar's support if the person is unreachable; processes vary.
  • Don't let it expire. Expired domains can be bought by others, and recovering them is hard and costly.

The simple rule

You should be able to walk away from any developer or agency, including us, and take your website with you. If that's not true today, fix it before you need it. It's also worth asking about when choosing a web design company.
