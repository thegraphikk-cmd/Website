For an NGO, trust or foundation, the website is often the deciding factor for donors, CSR teams and volunteers. Before giving, people want to know that you're real, registered, transparent and making a difference. A clear website answers those questions and makes giving easy.

What donors and CSR teams check

  • Who you are: mission, founders, trustees and team.
  • What you do: programmes, locations and who benefits.
  • Impact: numbers, stories and photos.
  • Registrations: legal status and tax-exemption details.
  • Transparency: annual reports and how money is used.
  • How to give: simple, secure online donation.

Show your registration details

Donors and companies look for these, so list the ones that apply to you, with registration numbers:

  • Legal form: trust, society or Section 8 company.
  • 12A/12AB registration and 80G approval, so donors know whether they can claim tax deductions.
  • CSR-1 registration if you work with corporate CSR funds.
  • FCRA registration, but only if you're registered to receive foreign contributions. If you aren't, say clearly that you accept donations from Indian citizens and residents only.
  • PAN and registered address.

Rules around these registrations change; confirm details with your CA before publishing.

Tell impact stories

Numbers matter ("2,400 children supported this year"), but stories move people. Share real stories with photos, with consent, and protect the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, especially children. Avoid identifying details where they could cause harm.

Make donating easy

  • A prominent Donate button on every page.
  • Suggested amounts linked to impact ("₹1,000 provides school supplies for one child for a year").
  • UPI, cards and netbanking through a payment gateway that supports donations; see setting up payments.
  • Monthly giving options; see recurring payments.
  • Automatic receipts, and 80G certificates where applicable, collecting PAN as required.

Transparency builds repeat donors

  • Annual reports and audited financial summaries.
  • A breakdown of how donations are used.
  • Regular updates: a blog, newsletter or project updates.

Volunteers and partners

A volunteer page with roles, time commitment and a sign-up form, plus a partners page for CSR collaborations with past partners (with permission), helps you grow beyond individual donations.

Get found

  • A Google Business Profile if you have an office or centre people visit.
  • Clear titles describing your cause and location.
  • Eligible non-profits may be able to apply for programmes like Google for Nonprofits; check current eligibility.

Keep it simple and fast

Many supporters will visit from a WhatsApp forward on their phones. A fast, simple site with one clear call to action, donate or volunteer, works better than a complex one. For budgets, see website cost in India.
