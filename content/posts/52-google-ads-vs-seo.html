When a small business wants more enquiries from Google, the question comes up quickly: should we pay for ads or invest in SEO? They work very differently, and the right answer depends on your budget, how quickly you need results and how competitive your market is.

Short answer: Google Ads gets you in front of searchers this week, but stops the day you stop paying. SEO takes months but keeps working. Most small businesses should do the SEO basics from day one, and use a modest, focused ads budget while organic rankings grow.

When Google Ads makes sense

  • You need enquiries now: a new business, a launch, or a slow season.
  • High-value services where one customer is worth far more than the cost of the clicks: real estate, clinics, education, B2B services.
  • Testing demand: find out which services and keywords convert before investing in content.
  • Very competitive searches where ranking organically will take a long time.

Costs per click vary hugely by industry and city, from a few rupees to a few hundred. Start small, track enquiries, and increase only what works.

When SEO makes sense

  • Long-term growth without paying for every visitor.
  • Local businesses, where Google Maps and reviews bring steady enquiries.
  • Question-led purchases, where customers research before buying ("how much does…", "which is better…").
  • Limited budgets, where you can invest time instead of money.

The most common way ads waste money

  • Sending ads to the home page instead of a focused landing page. See landing page vs website for ads.
  • Broad keywords that attract people looking for jobs, free courses or DIY tips.
  • No negative keywords such as "free", "job", "course" or competitor names you don't want.
  • No conversion tracking, so you can't tell which clicks became enquiries.
  • A slow mobile site that loses paid visitors before the page loads.

How they work together

  • Ads data informs SEO: keywords that convert well in ads are worth building content for.
  • SEO lowers ad dependency: as organic rankings grow, you can cut spend on those keywords.
  • The same website helps both: fast, clear pages improve your ad quality and your rankings.

A sensible starting plan

  1. Get the SEO foundations right: Google Business Profile, one page per service, fast mobile site, Search Console.
  2. Run a small, tightly targeted Google Ads campaign on your highest-value service, in your area, with conversion tracking.
  3. Publish helpful content and collect reviews every month.
  4. After three months, compare cost per enquiry from ads against organic growth, and rebalance.

Whichever you start with, the website is what turns clicks into customers. If yours is slow or unclear, fix that first.
