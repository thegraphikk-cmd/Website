The days before a website launch are when small mistakes slip through: a contact form that sends nowhere, a placeholder "Lorem ipsum" on the about page, or a site that looks great on a laptop and broken on a phone. Run through this checklist before you share the link with anyone.

Content

  1. No placeholder text: search every page for "lorem", "sample", "your text here" and "TBD".
  2. Spelling and grammar: read the home page and service pages out loud, or ask someone else to.
  3. Phone, email and address are correct everywhere, including the footer.
  4. Prices and offers are current, with GST mentioned where relevant.
  5. Images are yours or properly licensed, with no watermarks.
  6. Copyright year in the footer is right.

Forms and contact

  1. Test every form with a real submission and confirm the email or WhatsApp message arrives, and isn't in spam.
  2. Thank-you message: the visitor sees confirmation after submitting.
  3. Click-to-call and WhatsApp links work on a phone. See adding a WhatsApp button.
  4. Map shows the right location.

Mobile and browsers

  1. Check on a real phone, Android and iPhone if possible, not just a resized browser.
  2. Menus open and close; buttons are big enough to tap.
  3. No sideways scrolling; text is readable without zooming.
  4. Check Chrome, Safari and one more browser.

Speed

  1. Images are compressed and sized for the web (most under 200 KB).
  2. Run a speed test on the home page and a key service page. See how to test your website speed.

SEO basics

  1. Every page has a unique title and description that says what the page is about and where you are.
  2. One H1 heading per page, describing the page.
  3. The site isn't blocking search engines: check for "noindex" settings left on from development (a common WordPress mistake).
  4. A sitemap exists at /sitemap.xml, and robots.txt doesn't block it.
  5. Old URLs redirect to new ones if you're replacing a site; see redesigning without losing rankings.

Security, tracking and legal

  1. HTTPS works everywhere with no warnings; see why a site says "Not secure".
  2. Analytics and Search Console are set up and receiving data; see Search Console for beginners.
  3. Privacy policy and terms are published, and refund and shipping policies if you sell online.
  4. You own the logins: domain, hosting, website admin and analytics are in your business's name, with backups switched on.

On launch day

  • Submit your sitemap in Search Console and request indexing for the home page.
  • Update the website link on your Google Business Profile, Instagram, WhatsApp Business and email signature.
  • Tell your existing customers. A short WhatsApp message to past clients is often the first source of visits and enquiries.

After a week

Check that form enquiries are arriving, look at Search Console for errors, and fix anything visitors mention. A launch isn't the finish line; it's the point where real customers start using the site.
