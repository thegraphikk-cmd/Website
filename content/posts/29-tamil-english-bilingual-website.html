Most business websites in Tamil Nadu are English-only, even when the owner and most customers speak Tamil at home. For some businesses that's fine. For others, especially those serving local families, older customers or smaller towns, Tamil pages can build trust and reach people competitors miss.

Who benefits most from Tamil pages

  • Local services: clinics, schools, coaching centres, builders, home services and shops serving neighbourhoods.
  • Businesses in tier-2 and tier-3 towns across Tamil Nadu, where many customers prefer Tamil.
  • Businesses whose customers are older or less comfortable reading English.
  • Government, community and religious organisations.
  • Brands built on Tamil identity: food, culture, traditional products and media.

B2B companies, IT services and businesses selling across India usually do well with English, perhaps with a few key Tamil pages.

Do people search on Google in Tamil?

Many do, by typing in Tamil script, using voice search in Tamil, or mixing Tamil words in English letters ("Tanglish"). Search behaviour varies by audience, so check your Search Console queries and ask customers how they search. Even when people search in English, a Tamil option on the page can make them more comfortable contacting you.

Options for going bilingual

1. Full Tamil version

Every page available in both languages, with a language switcher. Best for businesses with a mainly Tamil-speaking customer base.

2. Key pages in Tamil

Home, main services and contact pages in Tamil; detailed pages in English. A good balance of cost and reach.

3. Tamil content within English pages

Key messages and calls to action in both languages on the same page. Simple, but less useful for search.

Setting it up properly for Google

  • Separate URLs per language, for example /ta/ for Tamil pages. Don't switch languages with a button that keeps the same URL.
  • hreflang tags linking the English and Tamil versions of each page, so Google shows the right version.
  • Set the lang attribute on Tamil pages (lang="ta").
  • Translate titles and meta descriptions, not just the body text.
  • Use real Tamil text, not images of text, so it can be read, searched and resized.

Translation mistakes to avoid

  • Raw machine translation. Automatic translation can produce stiff or wrong Tamil that hurts trust. Use it as a draft at most, and have a fluent speaker edit.
  • Overly formal Tamil that reads like a government notice. Write the way you'd speak to customers.
  • Forgetting buttons, forms and error messages, leaving the page half in English.
  • Letting the Tamil pages go out of date when prices or services change.

Fonts and design

Choose a Tamil font that's clear on mobile and pairs well with your English font. Tamil words are often longer, so test buttons and menus to make sure nothing overflows on small screens.

Our view

For local Tamil Nadu businesses, adding Tamil versions of your key pages is one of the more underused ways to stand out.
