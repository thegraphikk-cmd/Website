Online, your photos are your product. Customers can't touch, try or smell it, so the photos have to do the selling. The good news: a recent phone, a window and a few rupees of materials are enough for photos that look professional.

What you need

  • A phone with a decent camera (most phones from the last few years are fine).
  • A large window with indirect daylight.
  • A white or plain background: chart paper, a white bedsheet or a sheet of foam board.
  • A reflector: white foam board or even a sheet of thermocol to bounce light into shadows.
  • A small tripod or phone stand (optional, but it helps consistency).

Light is everything

  • Use daylight from a window, not direct sun. Morning or late afternoon light is softest.
  • Turn off room lights; mixing warm bulbs with daylight gives odd colours.
  • Place the product beside the window and the reflector on the opposite side to soften shadows.
  • Avoid the phone flash; it creates harsh shine and flat colours.

A simple home setup

Make a "sweep": tape a sheet of white chart paper to a wall or box so it curves down onto the table, with no visible corner. Place the product in the middle of the curve. You'll get a clean, seamless background that's easy to brighten later.

Shots every product needs

  1. Front view on a white or plain background, the main listing image.
  2. Back and side views.
  3. Close-ups of texture, fabric, stitching, ingredients or labels.
  4. Scale: the product in a hand or next to a familiar object.
  5. In use or on a person: lifestyle shots that show how it looks in real life.
  6. Packaging, especially if it's giftable.

For clothing, include a photo on a model or mannequin and mention the model's height and size.

Camera tips

  • Clean the lens. Fingerprints are the most common cause of hazy photos.
  • Use the main (1x) camera rather than wide-angle, which distorts products.
  • Step back and zoom a little (2x) for more natural proportions.
  • Tap to focus on the product and lower exposure slightly if white areas look blown out.
  • Keep the same angle and distance for every product so your store looks consistent.

Editing

Free apps like Snapseed or your phone's built-in editor are enough:

  • Crop to the same ratio for every product (square 1:1 or 4:5 portrait works for most stores).
  • Brighten the background to clean white and adjust white balance so colours are true.
  • Don't over-filter. Customers return products that look different from the photos.

Background-removal tools can help, but check edges carefully, especially for hair, fabric fringes and glass.

Prepare images for the web

Phone photos are often 3 to 6 MB each. Uploaded as-is, they make your store slow on mobile. Before uploading:

  • Resize to around 1600 to 2000 pixels on the longest side.
  • Compress to roughly 150 to 300 KB, ideally in WebP format.
  • Name files descriptively, such as blue-cotton-kurti-front.jpg, and add alt text in your store.

Shopify and most modern platforms resize images automatically, but starting with sensible sizes still helps. See speeding up a Shopify store on mobile.

Consistency sells

A store where every product has the same background, lighting and crop looks trustworthy, even if the photos are taken on a phone. Shoot products in batches with the same setup, and write down your settings so you can repeat them next month.
