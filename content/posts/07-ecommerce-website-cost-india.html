An online store in India can cost ₹10,000 or ₹10 lakh to build, and both numbers can be honest. The difference is the size of the catalogue, how custom the design is, and what the store needs to connect to. More importantly, the build is only part of the cost. This guide covers both the one-time build and the running costs that most quotes leave out.

Running costs every month or year

These are the costs that surprise new store owners:

  • Platform: Shopify charges a monthly plan fee; WooCommerce needs paid hosting instead. Check current prices on each platform's site.
  • Domain: roughly ₹800–₹1,500 a year for a .com or .in.
  • Payment gateway fees: typically around 2% per transaction for cards and wallets, often lower for UPI. Check your gateway's current rates.
  • Apps and plugins: reviews, GST invoices, COD fees, WhatsApp notifications and so on. These add up quickly on Shopify, where many apps charge monthly.
  • Shipping: per order, through an aggregator like Shiprocket or a courier directly. COD orders usually cost more to ship.
  • Returns and RTO: undelivered COD orders cost you shipping both ways. For some categories this is a significant cost.
  • Marketing: ads, influencer posts and email tools. Usually the biggest ongoing cost of all.

What drives the build price up

Number of products and variants

Fifty products with sizes and colours take longer to set up than fifty simple items. Large catalogues need bulk imports and careful category structure.

Custom design

A store designed around your brand, product photography and customer journey costs more than a customised theme, but usually converts better.

Integrations

Connecting your store to inventory software, accounting, an ERP or a warehouse adds development and testing time.

Indian requirements

GST invoices, COD rules by pincode or order value, and regional shipping rules all need setup and testing.

Cheap store vs professional store

A ₹15,000 store can work if you're testing a product idea. The common problems we see when brands come to us for a rebuild are:

  • Slow mobile pages from heavy themes and too many apps
  • Product pages with no real descriptions, which can't rank on Google
  • Checkout friction: forced account creation, confusing COD options
  • No conversion tracking, so ad spend can't be measured
  • The store or domain registered in the developer's name

A realistic first-year budget

For a new D2C brand with around 50 products on Shopify, a sensible first-year budget looks like this, excluding stock and marketing:

  • Build: ₹50,000 – ₹1,50,000 one-time
  • Platform plan and apps: depends on plan and apps chosen
  • Domain: around ₹1,000
  • Gateway fees: around 2% of online revenue
  • Small changes and support: ₹15,000 – ₹40,000
