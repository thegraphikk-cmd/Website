Marketplaces like Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho give you instant access to millions of buyers. Your own website gives you control, better margins and repeat customers. For most Indian brands the real question isn't "which one" but "which first, and how to balance both".

Selling on marketplaces

The advantages

  • Built-in traffic: buyers are already there and searching.
  • Trust: customers trust the marketplace's payments and returns.
  • Logistics: fulfilment programmes can handle storage, packing and delivery.
  • Fast start: list products in days, no website needed.

The downsides

  • Fees: referral or commission fees (often somewhere around 5% to 20%+ depending on category), plus closing, shipping and fulfilment charges. Check each marketplace's current fee card for your category.
  • Price competition: your product sits next to cheaper alternatives, often from the same supplier.
  • No customer relationship: you usually can't market to buyers again.
  • Rules change: fee changes, policy updates or a suspended listing can hit your income overnight.
  • Returns: generous return policies can eat into margins, especially in fashion.

Selling on your own website

The advantages

  • Higher margins: you pay platform and payment fees (a few percent), not marketplace commissions.
  • Your customers: you get their phone number and email for repeat orders, WhatsApp updates and offers.
  • Your brand: you control how products are presented, priced and bundled.
  • Long-term asset: every review, blog post and returning customer builds value you own.

The downsides

  • You bring the traffic: through Instagram, ads, Google and word of mouth.
  • Trust takes work: new buyers need reviews, clear policies and easy payment options.
  • You run operations: shipping, COD, returns and customer service.

The real comparison: margin per order

Take a ₹1,000 product. On a marketplace, after commission, fees and shipping, you might keep around ₹650 to ₹780, depending on category and programme. On your own site, after a payment gateway fee of about 2% and your own shipping, you might keep ₹850 to ₹900, but you may have spent money on ads to get that customer.

The difference is the second order. A customer who buys from your website can be reached again for free on WhatsApp or email. On a marketplace you pay the commission every time.

Which should you start with?

  • Start on a marketplace if you sell commodity products people search for by name, you have no audience yet, and you want to test demand quickly.
  • Start with your own website if you have a distinctive brand, an Instagram following or offline customers, or products that need explaining.
  • Do both once you're established: marketplaces for discovery, your site for loyal customers and better margins.

How to move marketplace buyers to your website

  • Use packaging and inserts that carry your brand name and website (within marketplace rules, which often restrict direct promotion inside packages).
  • Offer exclusive sizes, bundles or new launches on your own site first.
  • Build your Instagram so people search for your brand, not just the product type.
  • Make your website fast and trustworthy, with COD, UPI and clear returns.
