Before a payment gateway activates your account, it will check your website for certain pages: privacy policy, terms, refunds, shipping and contact details. These pages also protect you in disputes and reassure first-time buyers. Here's what each one needs, in plain English.

Note: this is a practical guide, not legal advice. Rules such as India's consumer protection e-commerce rules and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act set specific requirements, and they evolve. For anything unusual, have a lawyer review your policies.

Why these pages matter

  • Payment gateways like Razorpay, Cashfree and PayU typically require them before going live.
  • Consumer law: Indian e-commerce rules expect sellers to show clear information about the seller, returns, refunds and grievances.
  • Trust: buyers check return policies before ordering from a brand they don't know.
  • Fewer disputes: clear rules make chargebacks and arguments easier to handle.

1. Contact and business details

Show your legal business name, registered address, email, phone number and, where applicable, GSTIN. Many stores put this on a Contact page and in the footer. Hidden contact details are a red flag for customers and gateways alike.

2. Grievance officer

Indian e-commerce rules expect sellers to name a person who handles customer complaints, with contact details and a time frame for acknowledging and resolving complaints. For a small store this can simply be the owner. Put it on the Contact page or in the terms.

3. Privacy policy

Explain, in simple language:

  • What personal data you collect (name, phone, address, email, order history).
  • Why you collect it (processing orders, delivery, support, marketing if they agree).
  • Who you share it with (payment gateway, courier, analytics and marketing tools).
  • How long you keep it, and how customers can ask to see, correct or delete it.
  • How to contact you about privacy.

Don't copy another store's policy. It must describe what you actually do, including the apps and tools your store uses.

4. Refund, return and cancellation policy

The page customers read most carefully. Be clear about:

  • Return window: for example, 7 days from delivery.
  • Conditions: unused, with tags, in original packaging.
  • Exceptions: innerwear, personalised, perishable or sale items, if any.
  • Damaged or wrong items: how to report (photos or an unboxing video within 48 hours, for example).
  • Refund method and timeline: to the original payment method, store credit, or bank transfer for COD orders, within a stated number of days.
  • Cancellations: whether orders can be cancelled before dispatch.
  • Who pays return shipping.

5. Shipping policy

  • Areas you deliver to, and any you don't.
  • Dispatch time and typical delivery time by region.
  • Shipping charges and free-shipping thresholds.
  • COD availability and any COD fee.
  • What happens if a delivery fails or is refused.

See shipping for small online stores for how to set these up.

6. Terms and conditions

Covers the rules for using your store: pricing and errors, order acceptance, payment, intellectual property, limitation of liability, governing law and jurisdiction, and how disputes are handled.

Product pages count too

Each product should show the total price including taxes, any additional charges, key details like size, material and country of origin where relevant, and the return policy that applies. Surprises at checkout lose sales; see abandoned cart recovery.

Where to link them

  • In the footer of every page.
  • At checkout, near the pay button.
  • In order confirmation emails.

Keep them updated

Review your policies whenever you change couriers, return rules or add new apps that handle customer data, and at least once a year. Date each page with "Last updated". If you're setting up payments for the first time, read UPI, cards or COD: setting up payments.
