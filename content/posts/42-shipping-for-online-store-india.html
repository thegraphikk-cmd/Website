Shipping is where many new online stores lose money without noticing. A ₹499 product with ₹90 shipping, a COD fee and a return can end up costing more than it earns. Understanding how shipping is priced helps you set the right charges and choose the right partner.

Your two main options

1. Shipping aggregators

Platforms like Shiprocket, NimbusPost, iThink Logistics and others give you access to many couriers (Delhivery, Blue Dart, Xpressbees, Ekart, DTDC and more) through one account.

  • Pros: no minimum volume, easy setup, automatic courier choice, pickup from your location, COD remittance, plugins for Shopify and WooCommerce.
  • Cons: rates are higher than large direct contracts, and support goes through the aggregator, not the courier.

Best for: almost every new store, and most stores shipping under a few hundred orders a month.

2. Direct courier accounts

Once you ship consistently, couriers like Delhivery or Blue Dart may offer a direct contract.

  • Pros: lower per-shipment rates at volume, a dedicated account manager.
  • Cons: minimum volumes, more paperwork, and you lose the easy multi-courier switching.

How shipping is priced

Weight slabs

Rates are usually set per 500 g, with each extra 500 g charged again. A 520 g parcel is charged as 1 kg.

Volumetric weight

Couriers charge on whichever is higher: actual weight or volumetric weight. A common formula is:

Length × Breadth × Height (in cm) ÷ 5000 = volumetric weight in kg

A light but bulky box (a pillow, a lampshade) can cost far more than you expect. Use the smallest box that protects the product.

Zones

Prices vary by distance: within city, within state, metro to metro, rest of India, and special zones like the North-East and J&K, which cost more.

COD charges

Cash on delivery adds a fee per order, usually a fixed amount or a small percentage of order value, whichever is higher. COD also has higher return rates. See cash on delivery on Shopify for how to reduce RTO.

RTO (return to origin)

When a customer refuses a delivery, you usually pay the forward shipping and the return shipping. High RTO quietly destroys margins.

Setting your shipping charges

  • Free shipping above a threshold (for example above ₹999) lifts average order value and keeps small orders paying their way.
  • Flat rate (for example ₹60 everywhere) is simple and works when products are similar in weight.
  • Build shipping into the price for low-cost items, then advertise free shipping.
  • Extra charge for COD (a small fee) nudges customers towards prepaid UPI, which reduces RTO.

Whatever you choose, show shipping costs early. Surprise charges at checkout are a top reason for abandoned carts; see winning back abandoned carts.

Packaging tips

  • Weigh and measure each product packed, and enter these in your store so rates are accurate.
  • Use mailer bags for clothes and soft goods; they're light and cheap.
  • Branded tape or a thank-you card costs little and makes your brand memorable.
  • Take a photo or video of high-value parcels before dispatch, in case of damage claims.

Automate the boring parts

Connect your shipping account to your store so orders sync automatically, labels print in bulk, and customers get tracking links by SMS, email or WhatsApp. This saves hours a week and cuts "where is my order?" messages.

A sensible starting setup

  1. Sign up with one aggregator and connect it to your store.
  2. Enter accurate packed weights and dimensions.
  3. Offer free shipping above a threshold, with a small charge below it.
  4. Encourage prepaid orders and verify COD orders before dispatch.
  5. Review courier performance and RTO monthly.
