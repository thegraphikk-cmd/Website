Finding your Google Business Profile suspended is stressful: your listing may vanish from Maps, or you lose the ability to manage it. Most suspensions come from a handful of avoidable issues, and many can be reversed with a careful appeal.

Two kinds of suspension

  • Soft suspension: your profile still appears on Google, but you can't manage it. You'll see that the profile is suspended or that you've lost verification.
  • Hard suspension: the profile disappears from Search and Maps entirely, along with its reviews and photos.

Either way, Google usually doesn't say exactly which rule it thinks you broke. You have to check against its guidelines.

The most common reasons

1. Keywords in the business name

"Sharma Dental Clinic – Best Dentist in Anna Nagar Chennai" breaks the rules. Your profile name must match your real-world name, as shown on your signboard, invoices and website. Add descriptions to the description field, not the name.

2. Address problems

  • Using a virtual office, co-working desk you don't staff, or PO box.
  • Listing a home address as a storefront when customers don't visit you there. Service-area businesses should hide their address and set service areas instead.
  • Multiple profiles at the same address for the same business.

3. Business type doesn't qualify

Online-only businesses with no in-person contact with customers don't qualify for a Business Profile. You must either serve customers at your location or travel to them.

4. Suspicious edits

Changing the name, address or category several times in a short period, or edits by people you didn't authorise, can trigger a review.

5. Review violations

Buying reviews, offering incentives or posting reviews from staff can cause a suspension. See how to get reviews the right way.

6. Website or phone mismatch

A website that doesn't mention your business name, address or phone, or a phone number that goes to a call centre, can make your listing look less legitimate.

How to fix it, step by step

  1. Don't create a new profile. Duplicates make things worse and can lead to permanent removal.
  2. Read Google's guidelines for representing your business on Google, and honestly compare your profile against them.
  3. Fix the issues you can: remove keywords from the name, correct the address or switch to a service-area business, and align your website's name, address and phone with the profile.
  4. Gather evidence that you're a real business at that location:
    • Photos of your permanent signboard and storefront, showing the street.
    • A business registration, GST certificate, shop and establishment licence, or utility bill in the business name at that address.
    • A short video walking from the street to your premises, if asked.
  5. Submit an appeal through Google's Business Profile appeals tool (search "Google Business Profile appeals"). Upload the evidence and explain briefly what you corrected.
  6. Wait. Appeals can take from a few days to a few weeks. Check the status in the same tool, and don't keep editing the profile while it's under review.

If the appeal is rejected

You can usually appeal again with better evidence. Make sure every document clearly shows the same business name and address. If the business genuinely doesn't qualify (for example, online-only), focus on your website and other channels instead.

How to avoid future suspensions

  • Keep your profile name identical to your real business name.
  • Use a real, staffed address, or hide it if you're a service-area business.
  • Make big changes rarely, and only from your own account.
  • Get reviews honestly.
  • Keep name, address and phone consistent across your website, Justdial, Instagram and other listings.
