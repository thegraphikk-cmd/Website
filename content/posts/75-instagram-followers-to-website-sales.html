Many brands have thousands of Instagram followers and a website that barely gets visits. Likes don't pay bills; orders do. The gap is usually a few missing steps between seeing a product on Instagram and buying it on your site.

1. Fix your bio link

  • Link to your website, not a third-party link page with ten options, unless you genuinely need several links.
  • Point it to a "Shop from Instagram" page showing your current posts' products, new arrivals and best sellers.
  • Make the bio clearly say what you sell, who it's for and what makes you different, such as "Handblock cotton kurtis | Free shipping above ₹999 | COD".

2. Tag products

Where Instagram product tagging is available for your account, connect your catalogue (Shopify and other platforms can sync it) and tag products in posts and reels, so a tap shows the product and price with a link to your site.

3. Use Stories with links

Add link stickers to stories pointing to the exact product, not your home page. Keep highlights like "Shop", "New arrivals", "Size guide", "Reviews" and "How to order", each linking to the right page.

4. Make reels that sell

  • Show the product in use: how it fits, how it drapes, how it works.
  • Behind the scenes: making, packing, sourcing.
  • Customer reactions and unboxings (with permission).
  • End with a clear step: "Link in bio, search 'Indigo Anarkali'".

5. Make the website match the feed

If your Instagram looks premium and your website looks old and slow, people hesitate. Use the same photography style, colours and tone, and make sure the products people see on Instagram are easy to find on your site.

6. Make buying effortless on a phone

Instagram traffic is almost entirely mobile and impatient:

  • Fast-loading product pages; see speeding up a Shopify store.
  • Clear price, sizes, delivery time and COD info near the buy button; see product pages that sell.
  • Guest checkout with UPI and COD.
  • A WhatsApp button for quick questions.

7. Give followers a reason to buy on the site

  • Website-only drops or early access for followers.
  • A first-order code shared in stories.
  • Free shipping thresholds that encourage bigger baskets.

8. Reply to DMs with links

When followers ask "price?", reply with the product link, not just a number. Save quick replies with links to common products, the size guide and the order page. It turns a chat into a checkout.

9. Track what works

Use UTM links (for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=story) so your analytics show which stories, reels and bio links bring sales. See analytics basics.

10. Retarget visitors

Many people visit once and leave. Instagram and Facebook ads can show them the products they viewed. Start small once your tracking is set up; see Meta ads for small businesses.
