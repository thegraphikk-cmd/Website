Keyword research sounds like something only SEO experts do. In practice, it just means finding out which words your customers type into Google, so your website uses the same words. Here's a simple process using free tools.

Step 1: Start with what customers ask you

Before any tool, write down:

  • Your services and products, in the words customers use (not your internal names).
  • Questions you hear on calls and WhatsApp: prices, timelines, comparisons, "do you do…".
  • The areas and cities you serve.

Customers rarely search "bespoke culinary solutions". They search "catering for birthday party in Chennai" or "catering cost per plate".

Step 2: Use Google itself

  • Autocomplete: type your service into Google slowly and note the suggestions.
  • People also ask: the expandable questions in results are real questions searchers have.
  • Related searches at the bottom of the results page.
  • Who ranks now: look at the top results and Google Maps listings to see what kind of pages Google prefers for that search.

Step 3: Check your own data in Search Console

If your site already exists, Google Search Console shows the exact searches you already appear for. Look for searches with many impressions where you rank between positions 8 and 30. Those are the quickest wins: you're nearly there.

Step 4: Estimate demand with Keyword Planner

Google Ads' Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account (you don't have to run ads). It shows rough monthly search ranges for India or specific cities, and suggests related terms. Treat the numbers as directional; low-volume local searches can still bring valuable customers.

Step 5: Group keywords by intent

Learning"how to add business to Google Maps"How-to guide

One page should target one main intent. Don't try to rank one page for everything.

Step 6: Map keywords to pages

  • Each main service gets its own page, targeting "service + location".
  • Cost and comparison questions become blog posts.
  • Small questions become FAQs on the relevant service page.
  • Use the main phrase naturally in the page title, the H1 heading, the first paragraph and the description.

Don't overdo it

Repeating a keyword over and over ("best caterer Chennai, Chennai caterer best") makes pages worse for readers and doesn't help rankings. Write naturally for the customer, and cover the topic thoroughly.

Long-tail keywords are your friend

Short searches like "website" or "catering" are impossible for small businesses to rank for, and the people searching aren't ready to buy. Longer, specific searches ("vegetarian catering for 100 people in Anna Nagar") have fewer searchers, but they're much easier to rank for and much more likely to become customers.

Once you know your keywords, turn them into pages using content that converts and connect them with internal links.
