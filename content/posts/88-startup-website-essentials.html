For a startup, the website is often the first thing customers, investors and potential hires check. It doesn't need to be big, but it needs to make one thing instantly clear: what problem you solve, for whom, and why you're the team to solve it.

1. A sharp value proposition

The first screen should answer "What is this, and is it for me?" in a few seconds:

  • Headline: the outcome you deliver, in plain words. "Payroll for small Indian businesses in 10 minutes a month" beats "Revolutionising the future of work".
  • Subheading: who it's for and how it works, in one line.
  • One main call to action: book a demo, join the waitlist, start free.

2. Show the product

Screenshots, a short product video or an interactive demo. Abstract illustrations don't build confidence; seeing the actual product does. If you're pre-launch, show designs or a prototype.

3. How it works and key benefits

Three steps for how it works, and three to five benefits tied to customer problems. Keep features brief; link to a fuller features page if needed.

4. Proof, even early

  • Pilot customers or design partners (with permission).
  • Short testimonials or early metrics.
  • Recognition: incubators, accelerators, grants, Startup India recognition where relevant, and press.
  • Investor logos, once announced.

5. Team and story

Investors and early customers back people. A team section with photos, relevant experience and a short founding story adds credibility. Link to LinkedIn profiles.

6. Pricing, or a clear next step

If you can, show pricing. B2B startups often use "Book a demo", which is fine, but make the next step obvious and quick.

7. Trust and legal basics

  • Privacy policy and terms, especially if you collect sign-ups or data; see cookies, consent and the DPDP Act.
  • Security information if you handle sensitive data.
  • Company name and contact details.
  • A professional email on your domain; see business email.

8. Careers and press pages

A simple careers page helps hiring, and a press or media kit page (logo, founder photos, one-paragraph description) makes coverage easier.

9. Analytics from day one

Track sign-ups, demo bookings and where visitors come from. Early data shapes your messaging and marketing. See analytics basics.

10. Built to change

Your positioning will change, maybe several times. Choose a setup you can update quickly without a developer for every headline change, and keep the site fast. A focused, well-written site beats a big one you can't keep accurate.

Common startup website mistakes

  • Buzzwords instead of a clear explanation.
  • No real product visuals.
  • Several competing calls to action.
  • Slow, animation-heavy pages that frustrate mobile visitors.
