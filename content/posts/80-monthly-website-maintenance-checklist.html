A website isn't "done" at launch. Like a shop, it needs regular upkeep: things break quietly, information goes out of date and software needs updates. Here's a simple checklist to keep yours in good shape, in about an hour a month.

Every month

Security and backups

  • Check that automatic backups ran and are stored off-site.
  • Update the platform, theme and plugins (WordPress), or review installed apps (Shopify).
  • Remove plugins, apps and user accounts you no longer need.

See website security and backups.

Forms and contact

  • Submit each form and confirm the enquiry arrives (and isn't in spam).
  • Tap the WhatsApp and call buttons on a phone.

Content

  • Update offers, prices, opening hours and team changes.
  • Remove expired offers and events.
  • Publish at least one new piece of content if you have a blog.

Performance

  • Check Search Console for new errors or security issues.
  • Review visitors and enquiries in your analytics; see the five numbers to watch.
  • Update your Google Business Profile with posts and photos.

Every quarter

  • Speed test your key pages; see testing website speed.
  • Check for broken links; see fixing broken links and 404s.
  • Test a backup restore, or at least confirm you know how.
  • Review your top pages: are titles, descriptions and calls to action still right?
  • Check on several devices and browsers, especially after updates.
  • Review who has access, and remove former staff and freelancers.

Every year

  • Renewals: domain, hosting, SSL (if not automatic), email, premium plugins and apps. Make sure auto-renew uses a valid payment method.
  • Legal pages: update privacy, terms and refund policies; see legal pages for stores.
  • Footer year and any "since" or "years of experience" numbers.
  • Content refresh: update cost guides and yearly articles, and remove outdated pages (with redirects).
  • Design review: does the site still reflect your business? See signs you need a redesign.

Keep a simple log

A shared sheet with the date, what was checked, what was updated and any issues makes it easy to hand over to someone else, and to spot patterns.

DIY or a maintenance plan?

If your site is simple and you're comfortable with the admin panel, doing this yourself is very manageable. If your website brings in a lot of your business, or runs a store, a maintenance plan is often cheaper than fixing a hacked or broken site. See website maintenance cost in India.
