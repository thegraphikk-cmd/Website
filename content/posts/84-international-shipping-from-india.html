Indian products such as handlooms, jewellery, Ayurveda, spices, home décor and art have eager buyers abroad, especially among the Indian diaspora. Shipping internationally is more complex than domestic delivery, but small brands can start without a big export operation.

Note: export rules and tax treatment change and depend on your products. Use this as an overview and confirm the details with your CA and shipping partner.

Shipping options

  • Cross-border aggregators: platforms that offer international courier services for e-commerce with pickup, labels and paperwork support. The easiest way to start.
  • International couriers directly: DHL, FedEx, UPS and Aramex, often with better rates at volume.
  • India Post: cheaper for light parcels and slower, with export facilitation options for e-commerce.

Compare delivery times, tracking quality, rates per weight slab and how they handle customs.

Paperwork basics

  • IEC (Import Export Code): generally needed for commercial exports. Applied for online with the DGFT.
  • GST: exports can be zero-rated, typically by filing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) so you can export without paying IGST upfront. Discuss with your CA.
  • Commercial invoice with accurate product descriptions, HS codes and values.
  • Product-specific rules: food, cosmetics, Ayurveda, plants, wood and some materials face restrictions or extra certification in many countries.

Customs duties and taxes

Buyers abroad may have to pay import duties and taxes (VAT, GST or sales tax) depending on their country and the order value. Decide whether:

  • The customer pays on delivery (DDU/DAP), which is cheaper for you but can surprise customers, or
  • You collect duties at checkout (DDP), which is a smoother experience and more complex to set up.

Either way, state it clearly on your shipping policy and at checkout.

Pricing for international orders

  • International shipping is expensive. Price it in, use weight-based rates, or offer free shipping above a high threshold.
  • Show prices in the buyer's currency where your platform supports it.
  • Consider international-only bundles to raise order value.

Payments

Use a gateway that accepts international cards and, where available, PayPal or similar options. International payments may involve different fees and documentation (such as export-related records) with your gateway or bank. See payment gateways.

Store setup

  • Separate shipping zones and rates for India and international.
  • Size charts with international equivalents; see selling clothes online.
  • A clear international shipping and returns policy; see policy pages.
  • Realistic delivery estimates by region.

Start small

Begin with a few countries where you already have interest, such as the US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Canada or Australia, and a limited product range that ships easily. Learn from the first orders, then expand. For domestic logistics, see shipping for small online stores in India.
