Most small business websites say the same thing: "We are a leading company committed to quality and customer satisfaction." Visitors skim past it because it tells them nothing. Good website content is specific, written for the customer, and makes the next step obvious. You don't need to be a professional writer to do it.

Start with the customer, not the company

Before writing anything, answer three questions:

  1. Who is visiting? A parent looking for a school, a café owner who needs a menu website, a family planning a wedding.
  2. What problem do they want solved? In their words, not yours.
  3. What do they need to know to contact you? Price, timing, location, proof that you're good.

Then write as if you're talking to that one person.

The home page: answer "Am I in the right place?" in five seconds

Your top section needs:

  • A clear headline saying what you do and for whom. "Bridal makeup in Coimbatore, done in your home" beats "Unleash your beauty".
  • One line of detail: area served, speciality, or what makes you different.
  • One main button: "Get a quote", "Book a visit" or "Chat on WhatsApp".

Below that: your main services, proof (reviews, results, clients), how it works, and a final call to action.

Be specific

Specific details are believable; vague claims aren't.

Service pages: one service, fully explained

Each service page should cover:

  • What the service is and who it's for
  • What's included (and what isn't)
  • The process, in three to five steps
  • Price, or a price range and what affects it
  • Timeline
  • Proof: photos, a case study, reviews
  • FAQs and a clear way to enquire

Showing prices, or at least a "starting from", gets more serious enquiries and fewer time-wasters.

Answer objections before they're asked

Think about why someone might hesitate: "Is it too expensive?", "Will it take too long?", "Can I trust them?", "What if I don't like it?". Answer these on the page, in FAQs, guarantees or a clear process. Every unanswered question is a reason to keep looking.

Show proof

  • Reviews with names (with permission), ideally from Google.
  • Before-and-after photos or portfolio examples.
  • Numbers: years in business, projects completed, customers served.
  • Logos of known clients, if they agree.

The about page is about trust

People want to know who they're dealing with. Show real photos of you and your team, your story in a few paragraphs, and why you do this work. Skip the long "vision and mission" statements.

Write for phones and skimmers

  • Short paragraphs, two to three sentences.
  • Descriptive headings, so skimmers find what they need.
  • Bullet points for features and lists.
  • A button after every major section, not just at the bottom.

Use your customers' words

Look at the questions you get on WhatsApp and calls, your Google reviews and the searches in Search Console. Use the same phrases on your pages. It helps visitors and Google understand you.
