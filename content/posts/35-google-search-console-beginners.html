Google Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your website performs in search: which searches you appear for, how often people click, and whether Google can read all your pages. If you have a website and haven't set it up, you're flying blind.

Search Console vs Google Analytics

  • Search Console shows what happens on Google: searches, rankings, clicks, indexing problems.
  • Analytics shows what happens on your website: visits from every source, pages viewed, enquiries.

You want both, but for SEO, Search Console is the one to check first.

Setting it up

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with your business Google account.
  2. Choose a Domain property and enter your domain (for example yourbusiness.in). This covers every version: www, non-www, http and https.
  3. Verify ownership by adding the TXT record Google gives you at your domain registrar. It can take from a few minutes to a day to work.
  4. Open Sitemaps and submit your sitemap URL, usually yourbusiness.in/sitemap.xml.

Data starts appearing after a few days. Add your web developer as a user rather than sharing your password.

The four reports worth checking

1. Performance

The most useful report. It shows four numbers:

  • Impressions: how often your site appeared in search results.
  • Clicks: how often someone clicked through.
  • CTR: clicks divided by impressions.
  • Average position: roughly where you ranked (1 is the top).

Click the Queries tab to see the actual searches people used. This is gold: it tells you how customers describe what you sell, often in words you'd never have guessed.

2. Pages (indexing)

Shows which pages Google has indexed and which it hasn't, with a reason for each. A few common ones:

  • Page with redirect: usually fine, such as old URLs that point to new ones.
  • Discovered – currently not indexed: Google knows the page but hasn't crawled it yet. Common for new sites; internal links help.
  • Crawled – currently not indexed: Google read it and chose not to include it yet, often because it's thin or similar to another page.

3. URL Inspection

Paste any page address into the search bar at the top to see whether it's on Google. If you've just published or updated an important page, click Request indexing to add it to Google's priority queue.

4. Core Web Vitals

Shows whether real visitors find your pages fast on mobile and desktop. If pages are marked "Poor", read Core Web Vitals: what a fast site actually needs.

Quick wins from your data

  • High impressions, low clicks: your title and description aren't tempting enough. Rewrite them to match the search and promise a clear answer.
  • Ranking at positions 8 to 20: you're close to page one. Improve that page's content, answer the question more directly, and link to it from your other pages.
  • Searches you don't have a page for: write one. That's how a blog grows traffic.

A simple monthly routine

  1. Check clicks and impressions for the last 28 days against the previous period.
  2. Look at the top 20 queries for new opportunities.
  3. Open Pages and fix anything new marked as an error.
  4. Read any messages Google has sent about your site.

Fifteen minutes a month is enough for most small businesses. If your site is brand new, pair this with what to do in your first 90 days.
