A broken link sends visitors to a page that doesn't exist, usually a "404 Not Found" error. A few are normal on any site. But broken links on your key pages, or old addresses that used to rank, cost you visitors, enquiries and Google rankings.

Where broken links come from

  • Pages deleted or renamed without a redirect.
  • A redesign that changed page addresses (the most common cause).
  • Typos in links.
  • Links to other websites that have moved or closed.
  • Old links on social media, directories or printed materials.

When 404s matter, and when they don't

  • They matter when a page that had traffic, rankings or links from other sites now returns 404, or when your own menus and content link to missing pages.
  • They don't matter much for random addresses that never existed, or pages you deliberately removed that had no value.

Google doesn't penalise a site for having some 404s, but lost pages lose their rankings, and broken internal links waste visitors.

How to find them

Google Search Console

In the Pages report, look for "Not found (404)". Click through to see which addresses Google tried. See Search Console for beginners.

Crawling tools

Free tools and browser extensions can crawl your site and list broken links. Screaming Frog's free version, for example, checks up to 500 pages, which is plenty for most small business sites.

Your analytics

Check which visits land on your 404 page and where they came from.

How to fix them

1. Redirect old pages to the best match

If a page moved or was replaced, add a permanent 301 redirect from the old address to the most relevant new page, not just the home page. This passes most of the old page's value to the new one.

2. Fix internal links

Update menus, buttons and in-text links to point directly at the correct page, rather than relying on redirects. See internal linking.

3. Update or remove external links

Replace links to other sites that no longer work, or remove them.

4. Let truly dead pages go

If a page had no value and no good replacement, a 404 (or 410 "Gone") is fine.

Make your 404 page helpful

  • Say clearly the page wasn't found, in friendly language.
  • Offer links to your main services, blog and contact page.
  • Include a search box if your site is large.
  • Keep your branding so visitors don't think they've left your site.

Before a redesign

Export a list of all your current page addresses, especially the ones with traffic, and map each to its new address before launch. This single step prevents most ranking drops after a redesign. See redesigning without losing rankings.

Make it routine

Check for broken links every quarter as part of your maintenance checklist.
